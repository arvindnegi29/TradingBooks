## Symmetrical triangles: chart-level evidence and marking consequences

PDF110 Exhibit10.1 BA: two differently colored triangles occur in the middle of a wider chart, not as perfect schematic objects. Red C convergence sits vertically below later peak A/B relationship; blue D convergence sits under E less precisely in time. The apex can be a projected time marker but this is retrospective visual correspondence, not proof that time coordinates forecast a reversal. Blue boundaries visibly cut price, acknowledged109 as inferior fit. Do not silently optimize boundaries after breakout to manufacture an apex forecast.

PDF111 Exhibit10.2 ALK: large red triangle has three discernible contacts per boundary and a shrinking volume envelope, but plenty of internal whitespace due to its large size. The guidance allows scale-sensitive whitespace rather than requiring every portion of a triangle be populated. Mark upper and lower swings before intersection; the apex is A and subsequent peak is B. A label at apex is not itself breakout price.

PDF112 Exhibit10.3 GFF: earlier confirmed double bottom leads into a broad coiling triangle. There is an upward excursion/short release before the ultimate decline; therefore narrative about demand winning is not evidence of durable upside follow-through. Intervening chart events matter; recording only final direction loses the breakout ambiguity. Psychology112–113 is a hypothetical explanation of order flow inferred from price, not observed trader intent.

PDF113–114 Exhibit10.4 ASNA: A has three lower touches and only two upper; B depends on first lower touch (otherwise lower boundary horizontal); C widest part is near middle. Author nonetheless accepts A/B/C with caveats114. D would be too lopsided if central peak selected as start; E has adequate upper contacts but too much lower/interior vacancy. This establishes a judgment hierarchy: shape and distribution override mechanistic line fitting. Record borderline vs rejected, rather than pretend there is a fully numeric validity test.

PDF114 Exhibit10.5 HW: two widely separated lower anchors A/B plus two upper contacts can enclose a solitary mountain. C points into obvious empty area; inset strips lines off the price and makes the spurious identification clear. A scanner that tests only line convergence and 2+2 contacts would produce this false positive. Require alternating traversals and interior occupancy, not solely contact count.

PDF115–116 Exhibits10.6/10.7 MEA: unmarked exercise followed by answer chart includes triangles A/B/C, two double tops and H&S top. B's earliest upper spike is decisive; excluding it makes shape resemble ascending triangle. C lower contact count only two; D is a candle in a straight descent at a gap, not a local trough, and cannot increase swing-contact count. Mark gap and swing separately. Multiple pattern labels can coexist without assuming they trigger simultaneously.

PDF117–118 Exhibits10.8/10.9 VMC: answer chart includes big triangle B with numerous contacts, small A near Big W, and C/D region to right. Page116 warning says A candle is not minor low yet allows overall shape as triangle; the drawing actually circles A rather than drawing a clean pair of lines, underscoring ambiguity. C/D highs equal, so descending upper line does not exactly touch C; author tolerates mismatch. Annotator should show approximate fit and caveat, never falsify anchor coordinates. Descending triangle and later H&S bottom are distinct labels; Big W is double-bottom family. The source tolerates imperfect pattern shape without changing the definition of an actual swing contact.

PDF119 quiz: triangle has two sloped lines, pennants are short/pole-based, piercing boundary is insufficient. Confirmation is a state change, separate from shape candidate detection.

## Flags/pennants: detailed visual findings

PDF122 Exhibit11.1 CWTR: A–B is sharp upward pole with gap, B small near-sideways pennant, continuation toward C; C itself has another downward-tilting pennant. D much later is tiny descending-triangle-shaped pennant in downtrend. Blue flags in between have rectangular congestion geometry. Color is author annotation only, not market semantics. Same symbol can contain opposite-direction structures across time.

PDF123 Exhibit11.2 ABFS: A–B pole rises sharply; B–C slopes down but breakout at C continues down (reversal of incoming pole). D is retest/pullback along downward run; E reaches below initial launch level. Thus a bearish breakout from a bullish flag is not impossible. Later G–H upmove contains F as halfway congestion. Need distinguish consolidated shape, incoming direction, actual exit direction, and realized outcome as four fields.

PDF124 Exhibit11.3 HSY: early small blue flag and later red pennant around49.5; earnings/dividend annotation sits by strong run. Downsloping top plus flat bottom exemplifies pennant even though silhouette resembles descending triangle. Flat bottom49.5 is inferred support location in125, not actual known hidden buy orders. Claims about informed buying ahead of earnings are plausible story, not established causal evidence.

PDF125–126 Exhibit11.4 CKP: five blue flags and two red pennants illustrate bullish and bearish contexts. Insets draw multiple pennant orientations in uptrends/downtrends. Text says four additional variations but visually inset contains three schematic pennants in each box (six drawings); treat numerical count as inconsistent presentation rather than critical rule. Pennant A mediocre incoming rise resolves down; C begins after downward gap and irregular consolidation. A small knot can have ragged boundaries; mathematical symmetry not required. Insets illustrate continuations only, not all allowable exits127.

PDF126–127 Exhibits11.5/11.6 NST: answer marks eight flags. Flag5 is long, about month, with AB pole too small; weak proportionality makes it questionable. Page127 permits month-long consolidation if pole long enough; this qualifies strict less-than-three-week table122. Skill should prefer short duration but explicitly flag proportional exception, not apply contradictory absolute gates.

PDF128–129 Exhibits11.7/11.8 MAS: B is half-staff between A and C. D–E rise matches E–F decline, but time differs, so vertical amplitude equality does not imply equal time horizon. G attractive pennant but bottom-of-G to H reaches only about half incoming FG rise. J's inset shows flat top/upsloping bottom (small ascending triangle) and supports pennant classification based on short size plus pole; K loose but pole from J proportionate. The source itself contains partial moves and deviations; do not invent universal measured-move success probability.130 quiz disclaims always-halfway and daily-only assumptions; shorter pattern duration does not mean charts may only be daily/intraday.

## Double bottoms: detailed visual findings and temporal logic

PDF132 Exhibit12.1 HON: obvious AB twin trough after C; unrelated H&S top and later triple bottom are labeled elsewhere. Important to match each claimed reversal to its immediate inbound segment rather than entire chart trend.

PDF133 Exhibit12.2 LIZ: AB differs17c/about4% (132); red horizontal E line ends at first confirming close. Small AB combines into composite bottom C, giving larger C–D double bottom with its own higher confirmation line. GH,IJ,KL have distinct levels. Nested pattern scale matters: same candles may represent a minor entire bottom within larger candidate. Draw one level for each stated candidate, not a single global neckline.

PDF134 Exhibit12.3 CHG: early CD never confirms and is invalidated by lower A. Later A–B marks buy-support/reversal story, not validated proof that every participant behaves identically. Anecdotal purchase39.72 and sale54 plus dividend135 are one trade, not pattern expectancy. Blue triangle and broadening tops show context, not obligatory confluence conditions.

PDF136 Exhibit12.4 EXC: A Eve/Eve wide rounded troughs; B Eve/Adam mixes wide and sharply narrow; C Adam/Adam two narrow spikes. Vertical guides D/E show Eve broadening with height while Adam stays narrow. Classification depends on width profile over price range, not only exact number of bars at absolute low. No performance numbers given locally despite claim types differ135.

PDF137 Exhibit12.5 CRH: Adam/Eve example has short first bottom and broad second; ugly double bottom A–B has second bottom at least5% higher and confirms above intervening peak. This is a named exception to usual near-equal lows, not permission to relabel any rising lows without confirmation or inbound trend.

PDF138–139 Exhibits12.6/12.7 EXP: exercise explicitly ignores long green downward spike in May (136); that is pedagogical instruction, not a general data-cleansing rule. In production, do not delete anomalous bars without evidence of feed error. AB valid lower-right pattern confirms at C, yet subsequent chart falls: historical validity and profitable outcome differ. DE fails confirmation. FG has breakout but no proper downward inbound trend. Need both context and threshold crossing; either alone insufficient.

PDF140–141 Exhibits12.8/12.9 VMC: A accepted with1.7% difference; G narrow and accepted even though exercise excluded narrow patterns. A third trough after G confirmation does not revoke original double bottom. F lows1/2 do not confirm before3, so triple bottom; choosing2/3 alone ignores prior same-level1. Use actual event chronology and first-confirmation timestamp to classify double vs triple. P138 wording about excluding A despite1.7% difference is rhetorically confusing: author immediately says he still accepts it. Do not turn that aside into exact-equality rule.

## Triple bottoms: detailed findings, ambiguity, statistics

PDF144 Exhibit13.1 CHS: ABC confirmed triple with slightly higher A; DEF better double DE plus throwback F (F too high); HIJ pronounced low I is H&S. Candidate continuation/reversal context belongs to each grouping independently.

PDF145 Exhibit13.2 KWR: A/B/C narrow lows aroundsame level, E highest intervening peak, F first confirming close. Incoming D mountain helps establish falling context; D too far away for overshoot label, so don't assume any prior high is local overshoot. Volume rises, explicitly accepted.

PDF146 Exhibit13.3 ACI weekly: ABC broad relative to daily shapes; E/F confirmation; G tiny three weekly spikes also valid. H/I/J/K rise along sloping lower boundary of triangle and are not near-level triple bottom. L/M/N are labeled continuation due to rising entry, excluded from reversal class while acknowledged as possible continuation terminology147. Weekly view can aid scanning long structures but time aggregation can obscure short valleys.

PDF147 Exhibit13.4 BAX: earnings gap down and ABC around40, peak/trigger D around43, immediate throwback after confirmation. Volume falls across pattern. Later rise is known only retrospectively; institutional buying explanation148 cannot be derived from candle shapes alone. Text148 says price climbed to nearly43 from 'mid40'; visual is lows around40 and bounce43, so wording may be typo (mid40s would contradict stated direction). Preserve underlying chart evidence instead of repeating phrase as numeric rule.

PDF149 Exhibit13.5 PMTI and inset: A accepted despite higher middle; B loses by downward close first; C much lower middle is H&S; D formed during uptrend and is not reversal. Inset G/H entering from above red internal-peak line contrasts I/J entering from below. E has lower right bottom but accepted; F slightly lower middle still triple because inadequate distinct neck/head shape. These are fuzzy comparisons, not exact cent-based boundaries. Page151 caption says any bottom significantly higher/lower invalidates, while148 accepts high middle at A. Practical synthesis: reject clearly displaced lows/distinct H&S but label borderline cases, quantify deviations relative to pattern height and price scale, and cite discretion rather than absolute threshold.

PDF150–151 Exhibits13.6/13.7 TIE: A/B triple bottoms; two H&S examples have distinctly deeper head. Confirmation sometimes must be inferred by scanning beyond drawn cup; cup labels are geometry annotations, not entries.151 explicitly preserves confirmed triple B regardless of later crash/rally.

PDF152–153 Exhibits13.8/13.9 WIRE: A right bottom20c lower accepted150; B hard-to-spot triple and later extra valleys must be masked to inspect state at confirmation. C small, valid. Separate H&S has head much lower. Historical masking is useful for causal annotation: first mark with only bars available to that date, then reveal later outcome. A later extra low cannot retroactively invalidate a pattern already confirmed.

PDF154 quiz defines 'near' by price scale and visual similarity, not fixed cents; all three need not be exactly equal. Operational system must record scale, time frame and tolerances because author does not supply reproducible universal swing or similarity algorithm.

## Source error / implementation cautions

1. PDF144 sidebar says 'middle peak' below others when discussing triple bottoms; intended term is middle valley/bottom, established by143/148 and chart. Correct terminology in new notes; do not encode three peaks into a bottom classifier.
2. PDF122 row heading 'Two converging trendlines' covers both flags and pennants, while row body correctly distinguishes parallel flags. Follow body, not misleading shared heading.
3. Duration language varies (shorter than3 weeks122 versus no longer than3 weeks124, exception127). Store preferred duration and exception separately.
4. Triangle minimum2 versus preferred3 contacts is explicit110/115; accepted imperfect examples114/116 prohibit treating3 as universal necessity.
5. The author sometimes validates a silhouette despite imperfect swing anchors116. Mark geometry uncertainty honestly rather than silently moving candle extremes.
6. No explicit universal target or stop method in109–156. Lowest-low/highest-high preconfirmation invalidation is a pattern-state rule, not automatically a post-entry stop. Half-staff projections are possibilities with incomplete example outcomes. Any executable risk/target policy is a separately sourced extension and should be labeled as such.
7. All swing markings require awareness of when minor extrema become knowable. Source gives historical examples, not pivot-detection latency. A live scanner must define local-extrema lookback/confirmation and preserve as-of state to avoid hindsight.
