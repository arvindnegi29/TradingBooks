## Visual evidence, ambiguity and causality

- PDF301 and302 are the same Best Buy episode with different right edges. A/B are adjacent lows early in floor formation, C another low, E later floor visit. The later view adds a lower broadening bottom and subsequent failure. Chart extensions change what is known; avoid using302's future to label a live301 decision as certain. Earlier chapter text is required to decide if D is a valid initial breakout and whether the preceding move meets bust constraints.
- PDF306's EDE chart contains two small descending triangles on rising price and a later ascending triangle. The vertical apex alignments need not imply a top; examples include lows. Congestion makes C hard to judge, acknowledged by source. A system must preserve “minor high OR low near this date,” rather than assigning bearish direction because chapter title says sell.
- PDF307 EXC's large fall after B is a selected example and explicitly exceptional. The apex lies at trendline intersection C below B in price; projecting horizontally from C to a support level would be a category error.
- PDF310 FLIR news at C,D,E is narrative context. Litigation “could” be catalyst, and weak-government-demand announcement accompanies a later drop; this is not causal identification. Pattern geometry can be observed without attributing investor motives or claiming news caused the entire move.
- PDF311 combines multiple scales of trend and multiple lines on one weekly chart. A steep line can break while a shallower line survives; both must be distinguished in annotations. Line O's early warning was not a major reversal. J/K lower-peak comparison and M/N lower-valley comparison provide structure independent of chosen fitted support.
- PDF312 deliberate line offset is a source-picture exception essential for annotation fidelity. Copying blue A literally would produce a different close trigger from true triangle boundary. 313 says next opening fill and actual intended boundary.
- PDF316 and317 insets show two equal vertical heights across an intervening swing. They are not equal total leg distances from retracement trough to next peak or rally high to next trough. The projection includes recovery of original swing first, then another height beyond starting extremum.
- PDF320 LUV triangular breakdown fails. A technically consistent exit can still be followed by new highs. This case supports conditional language, exit-plan fidelity, and distinction between process correctness and favorable subsequent path.

## Statistical evidence and how it may be used

### Apex (305–307)

First study:500 stocks, July1991–July1996, average absolute time distance apex→minor high/low3.6days. Benchmark describes one month each side, peak/valley average spacing13.1days, half-spacing6.55days. This is an informal chance comparison, not a randomized or matched-control test; midpoint reasoning is not a full null distribution for random dates with irregular extrema. Two later tests use October2006–January2008: visual165/221≈75%, computer-drawn144/239≈60%. “Within a few days” is not a complete reproducible event definition; manual geometry and hindsight extrema may bias classification. Do not call60% a guaranteed lower bound or60–75% trade win rate. Counts measure alignment with any nearby minor high/low, not profitable sell signals, major trend reversals, or returns after costs. Four-day language alternates average distance and working proximity; it is not a proven hard window.

### Swing rule (315–316)

Source period March2000–May2011, >925stocks;32,042 uptrend samples,80% within±5% of projected price,86% for declines<10%. Downtrend24,620samples,67% within±5% in bull market,78% for swings<10%. The opening rhetoric says predicts “when” but the operational rule predicts PRICE, with no date formula. Percent tolerance may be broad relative to small swings, and many overlapping swings per stock can destroy independence. No precise swing-detection algorithm, holding interval, horizon, ex-ante anchor availability, survivorship controls, out-of-sample validation, cost model, or uncertainty intervals supplied in this segment. Do not transfer these rates to fixed-target reach probability or trading expectancy.

### Two-pattern strategy (321–322)

Initial database500stocks mid1991–mid1996 repeatedly inspected/categorized. In-sample July1991–July1996; out-of-sample1996–January2012 larger less-scoured database. Results explicitly BULL markets; historical $10 each-side commission. Open trades valued at latest available close, so realised and marked-to-market outcomes may mix. No explicit position sizing, portfolio capital constraints, overlap policy, liquidity/slippage, gap fills, corporate-action treatment or annualised portfolio returns provided here. Omission of ascending/symmetrical buys is a selection choice; validation must not pretend preregistration. “More trades” is offered as explanation for win rate decrease, but count alone does not establish why performance changed.

## Source quality, contradictions and transcription hazards

1. PDF329 extracted Eve&Adam double-top line can suggest A,A,E; visual clearly E then A. PDF332 TB labels crowd together. Spatial extraction order must not drive labeling.
2. PDF311 source's degree-based slope assertion depends on chart axis scale/aspect ratio. A skill should record shallow/steep relative to fixed chart scaling or price/time normalization, never infer physical45° as stable market measurement.
3. PDF312–313 line intentionally displaced. Annotation system should flag “illustration offset” and reconstruct true support from triangle boundary, not treat printed blue line as numeric ground truth.
4. PDF315 prose describes downtrend2→3 drop, but its equal-height construction is from old trough1 to projected trough3. The numerical example resolves ambiguity:target=2A−B. Uptrend analogous target=2A−B where A=preceding peak,B=retracement trough. Explicit variable semantics prevent a mistaken one-leg measured move.
5. PDF305–307 uses “four days on average” and “within a few days”; statement “at least60%” is extrapolated from two selected test outcomes. Preserve as historical author claim, not statistical lower-confidence bound.
6. PDF322 “win/loss” is percentage, not profit-factor ratio. Interpreting as59wins per41losses is plausible but table supplies no exact count. Larger out-of-sample N does not by itself explain lower win rate.
7. PDF320 extracted phrase “market was trendline lower” appears to be source wording/typographical error for trending lower; do not convert it into a technical named trendline rule.
8. PDF341 index lists Hurco on printed300 (PDF316), but actual HURC chart Exhibit28.2 is printed301(PDF317). Index is locator aid, not stronger evidence than page content.
9. PDF325–326 Bloomberg cheat sheet includes historical function identifiers and LIBOR context; these are source-era reference lists, not current functionality verified by this review. No chart-marking algorithm resides there.
10. PDF303 quiz says trading with trend means do not short while market is rising, not blanket “never short in bull market”; distinguish local market trend from broad regime. PDF314 false answer to an “only when” trend-change proposition reinforces that one structural test must not be made exclusive.

## Implications for a chart-reading/marking skill

Separate observed geometry, candidate pattern, confirmed breakout, failure/bust, projected level/date, and actual subsequent outcome. Every line needs anchors and timeframe/scale; every letter is exhibit-local. Preserve close-trigger versus next-open fill. Draw apex as time projection; draw swing target using starting extremum; do not invent entry/stop rules for glossary-only types. Record incomplete pattern candidates and ambiguous congestion instead of forcing a label. Make source statistics denominators, tolerance bands, market/time period, and outcome definition visible, and do not present them as current instrument probabilities. This segment supports conditional manual interpretation and historical context, not autonomous execution or live performance claims.
