## Page-specific visual and analytical observations

### Double tops: PDF157–166 (printed141–150)

- **157,Ex14.2 LMT:** chart puts A above narrow first crest and E above wider second crest; B is valley low, C close-below confirmation, D retrace to horizontal confirmation level, F start of preceding run. Horizontal B–D line is **valley support/confirmation**, not line joining peak tops. A blue arc highlights Eve's broader top. Earlier rectangle and descending triangle are secondary examples; triangle upper boundary falls and base is horizontal. Volume panel has its own descending blue guide. Need preserve price/volume panel separation in markings.
- **158:** B-close rule and D pullback explained. 58% pullback frequency from study of2,333 double tops, not probability of profit or target attainment. Market regime and Adam/Eve combination change comparative performance. Psychology section begins with company earnings/merger and hypothesized later institutional response.
- **159,Ex14.3 ZEP:** two approximately24 crests A and B, deep valley around19, earnings release E precedes large gap/drop to D around17; C near16 precedes steep run. Events visible in chart do not demonstrate which participant caused move. Text acknowledges confusing Adam/Eve labeling; full curvature width matters.
- **160,Ex14.4 POL:** AA label around early2010 identifies two narrow crests; Eve/Adam near spring2010 is wide rounded left top and thin right spike; later E/A around spring2011 again wide first/narrow second. Each pair occurs within broader rising history; subsequent small reversals do not promise terminal bear trend.
- **161,Ex14.5 ANF:** early2011 Adam/Eve looks superficially similar; Eve broadens lower on top. Mid2011 Eve/Eve pair around high70s uses arc spanning full left rounded crest. This is subjective taxonomy, not reliable single-bar classifier.
- **162–163,Ex14.6/14.7 COH:** inspect unmarked then marked chart. A is broad Eve/Eve near58, B narrow+wide near68, C wide+narrow near61. Four flags are small channel consolidations along steep poles, including a rising flag before later drop; right-hand symmetrical triangle has descending highs/rising lows. Visual scan tip starts with flagpole, then flag. Do not infer triangle confirmation from convergence alone.
- **164–165,Ex14.8/14.9 XLB:** A near33 shows attractive twin top but valley level B near32.5 is not closed below before rise, so rejected. C,D,E are confirmed pairs; F near36 is merely unconfirmed in visible sample. Marking common peak-level brackets alone conceals essential valley test.
- **166:** quiz establishes at least one intervening valley, two near-height peaks, and unconfirmed is not valid double top. Explicit distinction useful for status vocabulary: candidate versus confirmed versus invalidated.

### Triple tops: PDF167–177 (printed151–161)

- **167–168,Ex15.1 ALK:** A,B,C are three separate candidate formations, not the three peaks of one giant triple top. Red horizontal lines under each use lowest internal valley. A and B cross down; C never does and advances. Small postconfirmation drop at B illustrates confirmation does not guarantee magnitude.
- **169,Ex15.2 SXI:** A and C near30 flank lower/sharper B; approach E near21 rises into formation. Confirmation line is near25 and D marks break; pattern subsequently recovers strongly. Minor peak heights/shapes can differ. Blue volume guide slopes down across formation; unusual volume is expressly non-disqualifying.
- **170,Ex15.3 HAYN:** A,B,C cluster near38; long rising blue support line extends from prior year to later dates, distinct from triple-top confirmation. Labeled double top and flags precede it, D/E later recovery near sell price. Long narrative is hypothetical fund distribution/redemptions, not observable order-flow evidence.
- **171–172,Ex15.4 FOE:** earlier prominent central peak at roughly18 above shoulders distinguishes H&S; later ABC around14.5 is near-level triple top. Difference is visual prominence, not published numerical threshold. Text permits modest middle-peak elevation but advises against human-bust appearance.
- **173–174,Ex15.5/15.6 GPN:** H&S early2011, ABC triple top around54, later double top around52 with0.45(<1%) unequal peaks. D later peaks form descending diagonal; author avoids triple-top label because it often denotes triangle. Alignment tolerance must use context, not hard currency equality.
- **175–176,Ex15.7/15.8 TFX:** early small H&S can also reasonably be triple top because first peaks differ0.15. ABC large descending-price-span structure is rejected for nonconfirmation. D and E are actual triple tops; spike after D's third peak occurs **after** confirmation. July flag acts as reversal of uptrend; December flag and February pennant accompany advance. Large double bottom follows D decline.
- **177:** fourth-peak quiz clarifies event order; peak price proximity depends on chart scale, with no universal cent/% bound. A falling approach cannot be a reversal triple top. Overhead resistance interpretation not proof of future decline.

### H&S bottoms: PDF179–190 (printed163–174)

- **179–181,Ex16.1 SMRT:** A,B are valleys with central lower head; C is top with higher head. Shoulders need not be at same price; B demonstrates good approximate temporal symmetry. The printed definitions require well-defined three lows and two highs.
- **180,182,Ex16.2 CF:** AB valid formation includes armpit-high connecting declining neckline; shoulders have appreciably different levels but acceptable time balance. DEF appears superficially cleaner but left shoulder isn't five-before/five-after minor low and approach is rising, as chart yellow note explicitly says. A scanner cannot ignore approach trend or count every tiny wiggle. Daily minor-low criterion needs future bars and therefore detection lag in any causal scan; no contemporaneous hindsight labels.
- **181–183,Ex16.3 PFE:** downsloping neckline through armpits; annotate neckline on price panel and shoulder/head volume references separately. Psychology describes accumulation, but chart alone cannot identify smart money, their holdings, or motives. Downsloping-neckline bull-market performance statement is historical association.
- **183–184,Ex16.4 LLY:** three bottom examples, the right one complex with three left shoulder dips and two right dips. Only two of five dips strictly pass minor-low day-count, author still accepts complex shape. Need explicit exception note, not silent relaxation or claiming source offers exact machine rules. Other two examples allow higher left shoulder.
- **184–185,Ex16.5 TXI:** rising approach blue parallel channel challenges reversal classification; author concedes alternative longer-term decline framing. Upward red neckline A must be separated from horizontal blue right-armpit B; B is correct confirmation threshold. Neither reached before later close below bottom. This is rejected independent of disputable trend framing. Key operational prevention: don't indefinitely extrapolate a steep neckline.
- **186–187,Ex16.6/16.7 ANF:** A is continuation despite shape/confirmation; B and C reverse short downtrends. Horizontal red levels at A/B substitute right-armpit high for upsloping neckline. Display morphology and trend role as separate fields.
- **188–189,Ex16.8/16.9 COH:** A left-to-head time is much shorter than head-to-right-shoulder; rejected for asymmetry. B compact symmetric head/shoulders after sharp decline valid. Price-level similarity alone insufficient.
- **190:** quiz rejects touch/pierce as confirmation; close required. Neckline connects highs in bottoms; right (not left) armpit for upward neckline. Continuation occurrence admitted despite reversal definition.

## Causality, ambiguity, errors and limitations

1. **No causal order-flow inference:** stories on158–159,169–171,181–183,192–195 are illustrative hypotheses about investors/funds. Price/volume morphology cannot establish hidden buyers, earnings digestion, redemptions, or distribution motives. Preserve as possible mechanism, never scan finding.
2. **Pivot lookahead:** five bars before/after minor-low language on180,183 requires right-side observations. Backdated pivot mark must carry first-known timestamp at least five bars later; the source does not supply an implementation for intraday/weekly conversion.
3. **Role versus validity tension:**179/191 define reversals;186–187 and197–198 admit continuation variants. Represent shape, confirmation status, and trend role independently. Do not automatically erase morphology merely because approach direction differs; do not report continuation as a validated reversal.
4. **Soft symmetry:** no numeric shoulder/time ratio or triple-peak tolerance supplied. Author accepts unequal shoulders, lower middle peaks, outlier narrow pivots; rejects extreme imbalance. Exact thresholds invented for automation must be labeled implementation assumptions and validated separately.
5. **Potential source/diagram issue,169:** prose says volume downtrend blue line shown; full-page rendered Ex15.2 has no clearly visible blue downward volume guide at1100px. Do not assert visible anchor coordinates for it. Text rule itself clear.
6. **Page157 F marker ambiguity:** text identifies start at F; small F annotation not clearly legible in rendered chart, unlike A–E. Avoid extracting an exact F date/price from this figure without higher-resolution inspection/data.
7. **No trade execution policy:** identification examples do not specify spreads, slippage, next-bar entry, order types, exchange session, stop placement, target formula or sizing. Validation close does not imply fill at that close. No precise target/stop may be attributed to this assigned range.
8. **Statistics:**158 reports2,333 double tops/58% pullbacks and comparative market/subtype performance;159 near-doubling failure-rate comparison omits absolute bases here.169 duration and middle-volume tendencies;171 equivalence with Adam/Adam;180–181,192–194 comparative H&S volume/neckline/shoulder performance. None supplies full study period, exact sampling universe, costs, confidence intervals, selection protocol or failure definitions in these pages. Never transfer to BTC/Indian options or live probability without new evidence.
9. **Strong caveat,203:** PartIII introduction explicitly says ensuing chapters use bull-market data and hundreds of perfect trades, warns actual results need not match, and numbers can change with added samples. It prefers understanding concepts/context over performance figures, emphasizes market/industry/stock context, and notes setup discussion is incomplete. This caveat should be prominent in any source-based skill.
10. **Boundary:** all range text and visuals reviewed; range begins mid-double-top chapter and ends before actual Basic Buy Setups chapters. Full double-top identification earlier and later trading methods require other agents' evidence. No claim of complete-book verification from this review alone.
