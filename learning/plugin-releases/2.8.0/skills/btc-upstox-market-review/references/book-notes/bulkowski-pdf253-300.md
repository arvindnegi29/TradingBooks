## Most important implementation implications

1. Maintain separate states: geometric candidate, confirmed original breakout, adverse boundary break, fully busted pattern. Chapter25 explicitly relaxes the definition for its pedagogical examples (PDF287). Do not let its adverse-break examples overwrite the strict bust state machine.
2. Confirmation usually depends on a **close**, while stop-entry examples use an intrabar buy-stop above a line. A chart annotation must identify which event is being drawn. Price touching a line is neither a close-confirmed breakout nor a fill guarantee.
3. Projection geometry differs for head-and-shoulders: measure vertically between head and neckline at the head's date, then project from actual neckline breakout. Do not use the full pattern bounding-box height (PDF275–277).
4. Targets are estimates with historical hit frequencies, not promises. Underlying support may delay price, fail, or be irrelevant. Seven before/after sell examples deliberately include immediate recovery and target failure.
5. Sampling conditions matter: bull-market stock studies, perfect hindsight exits, and retrospective subgroups cannot be converted to live crypto/Indian-options probabilities. Single-bust superiority is especially noncausal because future double/triple busts are unknowable at entry.
6. Preserve contradictions and flag them. PDF291 says less than1% for a busted double bottom while PDF287 gives less than10% generally. PDF296 calls a rectangle a triangle. PDF266 quiz rejects whole-number S/R despite PDF260 explicitly listing round numbers. These are source issues, not rules to encode silently.

## Causality, precision, and source handling rules for the proposed skill

- A scanner should record exact timeframe, log/linear setting, currency, adjusted-data status and line anchors before marking. These pages switch daily/weekly/monthly and use cheap-share percentage traps. No tolerance in pixels can substitute for price/time tolerances.
- Separate level role labels: original confirmation, failed-break boundary, strict-bust threshold, target, scenario support, trailing stop, and entry execution estimate. One blue horizontal line may be contextual support while a red line is confirmation; color meaning is not universal across exhibits.
- Trend start uses20% reversal definition; monthly ultimate-high identification uses high-to-close20% drawdown; strict bust uses<10% excursion. They are different denominators and events. Store formulas explicitly.
- Do not identify 'no throwback', 'single bust', 'ultimate high', final support that held, or terminal breakout as live-known features. Mark them retrospective only or display pending.
- Price-based support is observational. News and 'smart money' explanations in the source are narratives. A chart does not establish who sold, knew earnings, caused a move, or whether a support circle prevented an even larger decline.
- Full versus fractional height target frequencies are not probability multipliers. Multiplying height by53%/55% gives a nearer heuristic, not53%/55% chance of profit or an expected return.
- The monthly system's 'no stops' statement conflicts in ordinary language with its gain-floor/20% exit rules. Preserve the specific setup's rules and label delayed monthly exits; avoid universal no-stop instruction.
- Penny entry/stop offsets are historical U.S.-stock examples, not portable tick-size instructions. In new markets use venue-valid tick and model gap/slippage. A protective stop limits planned risk but cannot ensure a penny-below fill after a gap.
- All performance tables here need provenance metadata, pattern direction, market condition, target denominator, holding/ultimate-high definition, sample count when given, and 'historical perfect-trade comparison' qualification. Do not present the percentages as fresh estimates or validated execution outcomes.
