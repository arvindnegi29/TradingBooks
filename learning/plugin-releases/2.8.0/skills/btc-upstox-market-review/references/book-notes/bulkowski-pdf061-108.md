# Bulkowski source evaluation: physical PDF pages 061–108

Source: `Visual Guide to Chart Patterns 2012.pdf` (file title; parent review notes copyright page says 2013). All references below use physical PDF page numbers, not the printed numbers. Physical 061 is printed 45; 108 is blank after printed 91. This is original analytical commentary, not reproduced source text or imagery.

## Coverage and method

Read the complete extracted text of physical pages 061–108 in bounded, untruncated batches (061–072,073–084,085–096, then 097–099 and100–108). Visually inspected all 48 pages via twelve four-page contact sheets. Further inspected enlarged chart regions for 064,065,066,077,081,092,094,098,099,102,104,106. All substantive charts, diagrams, tables and exercise answers in this range were inspected. Blank pages 062,070,096,108 were visually verified; 069 is a part divider. No external market data, backtests, plugin modifications or live calls were performed.

## Detailed source-grounded observations

### 061: gap quiz carried over from prior chapter

The answers reinforce that closing means subsequent filling of the price discontinuity, not a fixed month deadline. Breakaway gaps can have high volume, leave congestion and remain open; exhaustion gaps tend to fill quickly; continuation gaps are not invariably midpoint events. This page alone does not provide enough new geometry to rewrite all gap identification rules. Treat the selected answer list as a recap of its preceding chapter.

### 063–068: return loops and stop placement

063–064 / Exhibit6.1: AMTD has a small ascending triangle; A is initial upward breakout near16.5–17, D prior minor low below the entry, C the post-breakout plunge and recovery, B later high around23. The chart's wide later advance makes the stop-out lesson visually vivid but does not make that later high knowable at C. The earnings explanation is source narration; OHLC bars alone do not establish the announcement's causality. A buy-stop trigger one cent above the triangle top is an illustrative order method and can occur intrabar; keep that distinct from the chapter's formal close-based definition of breakout.

064–065 / Exhibit6.2: UIL A is purchase around triangle resistance near27, B low stop reference below triangle, C deeper post-breakout low near25.5 and D later high around36. Price first breaks downward, then returns and rises. This qualifies under the author's pullback convention, despite the owner initially being long. Avoid labeling it throwback because the final outcome is upward. Percent gains32%/38% in these stories are retrospective price moves, not attainable net trade returns.

065 table: original pattern breakout, a continuation leg, an actual loop and white space, and a return within30days are the identifying sequence. Boundary may be diagonal; mark actual line value at return time rather than only a static horizontal level.

066 / Exhibit6.3: left schematic places A during upward departure, B at peak after average6days/+8%, C at return after average10days total, D on subsequent rise. Right places E on downward departure, F at low after6days, G at11day return, H on resumed decline. Graphic says4%–10% initial pullback drop; narrative does not give one universal average percentage. Diagrams use stylized bars, not measured price series. The arrows65%higher and47%lower describe aftermath conditioned on a return event; they are not unconditional probabilities of profitable entries.

067 table: historical throwback/pullback occurrence by family: ascending triangles60%/56%; descending50%/55%; double bottoms56%/N/A; double topsN/A/57%; H&S bottoms57%/N/A; H&S topsN/A/59%; rectangle bottoms59%/59%; rectangle tops64%/54%; symmetrical triangles58%/58%; triple bottoms58%/N/A; triple topsN/A/63%. N/A means not applicable under classification, not zero frequency. Source describes thousands of observations but range supplies neither counts per row nor confidence intervals nor full sampling rules. Rates should remain historical descriptive context, not current BTC/intraday forecasts. Of returns,65%of throwbacks resume upward and35%close below bottom;53%of pullbacks close above top and47%drop. These opposite outcomes make 'wait for retest' an incomplete strategy without invalidation and risk rules.

### 071–084: rectangles and tolerances

071–072 / Exhibit7.1 ACMR: red horizontal ceiling and floor with upper touches1–3/lower4–6. Inbound trend down makes it a bottom although exit is down. Mark breakout only on outside close. Volume shrinks before breakout, but this is tendency, not mandatory signal. Study1228daily rectangles1991–2011 averaged71days: average is not a minimum/maximum duration filter.

073–075 / Exhibits7.2 BSET and7.3 AGU use weekly charts. BSET's upper line is deliberately internal through some bars to communicate repeated horizontal resistance, not an absolute all-bars envelope. Upward inbound trend makes top. AGU falls from A into a bottom despite preceding longer rise B and an undershoot F; ignore isolated immediate pre-pattern detour when classifying entrance. Rising volume D/E is compatible with rectangle-bottom upward exit. Similar inbound/outbound price velocity is a visual heuristic, not a quantified equality or robust ranking rule; raw cents/day also depends on share price, timeframe and splits.

074–076 / Exhibit7.4 FRD: narrated institutional accumulation/distribution offers intuitive supply-demand mechanism for fixed high/low range. Treat named actors as hypothetical explanation, not inferred identities. Top/bottom boundaries are zones (narrative buy5.50/sell5.75, observed floor5.45/ceilingnear6). Breakout C has40700shares against12200prior day; one example of volume expansion cannot establish required relative-volume multiplier.

077–078 / Exhibit7.5 ARWR: top touches cluster at B but C supports far end. Author suggests lowering ceiling toward F, internal if necessary. Floor at isolated D is incorrectly anchored; lift toward recurrent valleys E. This is central annotation lesson: do not maximize enclosing range at cost of ignoring repetitive structure. Revised shape still imperfect, with flexible boundary placement. Capture alternative levels and confidence rather than present exact resistance as uniquely determined.

078–079 / Exhibit7.6 GLBL: post-buyout flatline A looks geometrically like congestion but is excluded because corporate offer fixes price. Geometry-only scanner should flag context unknown rather than confidently call this a rectangle. Event lookup or supplied news may resolve, but do not invent news from appearance alone.

079–081 / Exhibits7.7/7.8 DPL exercise: red rectangle bottom has highs1–5 and lows6–11. Early peak1 and valley8 slightly protrude but repeated later contacts support internal boundaries. Blue CED top may have first lower contact B on straight run; author calls it minor low though hard to discern, an acknowledged subjective edge. J is rejected: bottom is V-shaped with only one contact; H rests on slope rather than genuine peak and I is not a true minor high. Distinguish contacts from all boundary intersections.

080–083 / Exhibits7.9/7.10 OLN: right red C is bottom; blue D/E are tops, with B overshoot into D. A on hillside cannot count as minor high. D/E can reasonably be one longer rectangle rather than separate patterns. Explicitly preserve nested/merged candidates instead of forcing unique segmentation.084 quiz reconfirms near-horizontal boundaries allowed and pierce alone insufficient.

### 085–095: ascending triangles and classification instability

085–086 / Exhibit8.1 LXU: rising lows and nearly level highs lead upward breakout B, then failure C under bottom. A is pre-pattern overshoot, irrelevant because this family has no top/bottom subtypes. D breakout volume below average is not disqualifying. Formal bust requires less10%excursion before opposite-side close; don't call every eventual reversal a bust without checking excursion history.

087–088 / Exhibit8.2 FOE: highs A–D and rising lows E–G demonstrate repeated traversals. Initial downward break then opposite upside break is a bust but does not lead sustained gain. Thus 'busted' is a state classification, not a promise of powerful move. Author conjectures prior extended rise may explain weakness; this is not causal evidence.

089 / Exhibit8.3 GD weekly: three contacts upper A and lower B; some white space C tolerated. H&S bottom labels head/LS/RS are context examples, not full H&S rule set. Historical2011research >1600ascending triangles, both bull and bear markets,64%upward breaks. Contrast06367%bull-market claim: not contradictory because population differs. No current/regime-specific transfer established.

090 / Exhibit8.4 CWTR: formally two top contacts and multiple bottom contacts are insufficient; large empty interior exposes price mostly following one side then making a late excursion. Reject despite valid-looking outer wedge.

091–092 / Exhibits8.5/8.6 TREX: apex-time correspondences A/B,E/F,G/H mark where price turns above/below future intersection time; no known duration of turn. Middle C/D rising-top candidate better considered rising wedge. Blue redraw appears horizontal, changes breakout direction versus red line. This directly demonstrates that subjective boundary placement changes both classification and signal: report alternative, do not choose line retroactively to fit outcome. Bottom endpoint crossed during plunge does not count as support touch. Nearby broadening top/H&S/double top labels are eye-training context.

093–094 / Exhibits8.7/8.8 CX: author labels February/April triangles with question marks. Both share nearby price structure; chart also labels H&S reversal patterns and double bottom. Keep ambiguity in a chart-marking skill; do not silently upgrade '?' into confirmed textbook examples.095 quiz treats slight top slant and likely apex turn as valid, but supplies no operational tolerance or accuracy estimate.

### 097–108: descending triangles and overlap

097–098 / Exhibit9.1 NWY: valleys A–C create horizontal supportnear2 and peaks D–F declining resistance. Initial downbreak reverses and eventually closes above resistance; G volume trend declines then spikes at break. Suggested strong rebound after swift prior drop is qualitative. Earlier H could have rising bottom and be symmetrical triangle; author accepts both readings. Context labels double top/rectangle tops should not be interpreted as additional formal definitions.

098–100 / Exhibit9.2 MAS: triangle A has three low contacts, falling B high line; volume C declines. E shows U-shaped volume and remains valid. F–J can be descending triangle despite only two high contacts and less-straight bottom. Recommended five total contacts is preference, while107 quiz minimum is four total. Unmarked November2009–January2010 triangle has three each and upward break: useful scanner recall check, avoid downbreak-only detector.

100–101 / Exhibit9.3 CX: lows A/B/C near5 and highs D/E/F/G decline into support break. Study1166descending triangles yielded64%downbreaks. The thought experiment attributing exact orders to institutions is explanatory fiction; September pressure claim does not prove seasonality caused this break.

101–102 / Exhibit9.4 EXPD: A/B provide matching lows and declining top reaches C, apparently valid outer geometry; interior gap makes it invalid. Red inset D provides repeated crossings; blue inset without boundaries reveals rise-and-fall instead of repeated compression. Scanner should inspect raw chart and alternative anchor choices; author advises skipping if other observers unlikely to see same form.

103–104 / Exhibits9.5/9.6 NOC: large triangle with three highs and two lows, plus overlapping double bottom. Large absolute white area is acceptable because several full traversals exist and width makes crossing take time. Whitespace must be assessed relative to oscillation structure/time, not fixed pixel area. Double bottom and triangle can both be valid simultaneously.

105–106 / Exhibits9.7/9.8 CACH: red A valid small descending triangle; blue B invalid large-gap candidate; blue symmetrical triangle later has falling highs and rising lows, with no directional shape bias asserted here. Other double tops/bottom serve visual recognition.107 quiz minimum four total touches, five preferred.108blank.

## Errors, limitations and integration recommendations

- Arithmetic error074–075:16,000average daily shares,1%claimed as1600shares; correct is160. Do not preserve mistaken arithmetic. The1%liquidity heuristic itself is source opinion without impact study in this range.
- Text extraction order can split tables around charts; complete text+visual review avoids confusing characteristics table continuation with narrative. Source figure captions/printed page numbers differ by16from physical references here.
- Author permits internal lines, small slopes, protrusions and overlapping family names, yet gives no numerical tolerances. An implementation must document its own tolerance choices as extensions; never attribute invented ATR thresholds, exact touch percentages or minimum swing lengths to this text.
- Pivot identification inherently may require subsequent bars. Historical exercises show completed patterns and later outcomes. A live scan must date when each pivot/boundary became observable, keep unfinished candidate versus confirmed breakout separate, and prevent later candles from redrawing earlier signal without disclosure.
- Mark resistance/support as zones when price mismatches anchors; list actual swing dates/prices and line equation, timeframe, data scale, first confirming close, and ambiguous alternatives. The charts are selected historical US equities on daily/weekly scales. Shape recognition across timeframes is asserted but statistical performance is not established across intraday/crypto instruments.
- These are identification chapters with return-loop discussion; they do not supply a complete entry/stop/target/position-sizing system. Annotated chart plans need explicit separate evidence for those components. A historical directional frequency is not probability of hitting target before stop, nor net expectancy after costs.
- Avoid causal claims from OHLC geometry. Supply/demand narration is a plausible mechanism, not observable actor-level proof. Failure examples undermine deterministic pattern trading and support honest uncertainty labels.
