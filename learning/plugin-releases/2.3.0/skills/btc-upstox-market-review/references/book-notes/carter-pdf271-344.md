## Chapter12 — anchor charts and waves (PDF271–277)
- Anchor principle: review higher-timeframe directional context before lower-timeframe entry; hourly bearish squeeze can coexist with misleading five-minute bullish consolidation (271–272).
- A/B/C waves nominally short/medium/long trend. Author estimates about6/15/30 bars but explicitly says inventor Rodney Julian does not disclose specifics (272). These are NOT an implementation formula; do not recreate TTM Wave as a simple moving average or claim exact detection.
- Author prioritizes C-wave side of zero. Squeeze direction and A/B/C agreement distinguish aligned continuation from countertrend. In BIDU example (274–275), C positive prevents taking short squeeze; A unable to rise above zero weakens long despite C positive. These filters share underlying price data, not independent confirmations.
- Example management: exit half on momentum loss, stop remaining at entry, retain while C below zero for short (272). Countertrend squeeze example target1ATR vs greater trend-following ambition (275). Neither is universal or validated.
- Early longer-timeframe squeeze entry (276–277): once first black squeeze dot appears, B/C above zero and all waves rising; A may be below zero if rising. Author claims90% chance of long release with no sample/error bars provided. Label as author's unvalidated historical assertion, not forecast probability.
- Weekly GLD example: May ITM calls entered in fifths on successive weekly observations before final squeeze release; author illustrates eventual half exit at momentum loss and remaining C-wave-based management. This is risk aggregation, not a license to average into losing trades or use fixed20-contract size. IV, expiry, execution and total cash-risk limits needed for any adaptation.

## Chapter13 — TTM trend bars for exit (PDF278–281)
- Smoothed bar-color context considers prior six bars; narrative compares average price to upper/lower half of trading range (278–279). Definition does not fully specify price averaging, range, current-bar inclusion or equality conventions. Do not equate exactly with Heikin-Ashi or publish an unverified replica.
- For a squeeze long: take half at momentum deterioration, move stop to entry, exit remainder only after two consecutive opposite TTM trend bars (279). Short mirrored. Intended primarily for exit management, not entry generator (281).
- Visual isolated scary candle may occur without two opposing smoothed bars, so avoid discretionary premature exit. However smooth color does NOT remove stop/gap risk and intent to 'shake out' traders is author interpretation, not provable order-flow evidence.
- Most discussed indicators proprietary, some platform built-ins/downloads (281); availability and behavior not verified now.

## Chapter14 — scalper alerts (PDF282–304)
- Narrative: three consecutive higher closes for reversal buy, lower closes for sell, not merely three higher highs/lower lows. Retroactive paint marks first bar only AFTER third closes. Explicit hourly example PDF284:10:30 bar marked at12:30. A historical first-bar marker is not a tradable entry timestamp.
- Critical algorithm ambiguity (282): additional filter ignores lower-close sequences failing to break signal-bar low; long trigger described as first bar with higher low than prior, followed by close above trigger high, painting prior low bar. This is not identical to unconditional three-close logic. Need named, explicit algorithm specification before detector/backtest; separate simple-three-closes research variant from exact proprietary TTM behavior.
- Stated rules:24h intraday input (regular-session daily), buy market at third close, exit/opposite position at three lower closes; author's intraday method has no fixed stop, signal-only exit. Daily stop is low of first bar in buy sequence (high for short). For≤15m close by16:10 Eastern;≥60m retain overnight until reverse. These are historical US-specific parameters and unsafe to silently apply to leveraged perpetuals. Keep defined cash risk/protective invalidation and no automatic position reversal.
- Tick bars count trades, not size/volume, e.g.233 trades/bar. Examples:ES233 for scalps/987 for intraday swings, YM144, generic377 for active commodities/stocks (282). Trade-history/feed conventions matter; cannot derive true tick bars from OHLCV minute candles.
- Examples across ES/YM/FX/stocks/crude are illustrations, mostly winners, not complete track record. USD pip values require actual lot/contract and conversion, not historical dollar numbers. PDF304 suggests pivot + scalper combination, but shared inputs do not yield independent evidence or quantified combined probability.
- Source issues: PDF290 item6 says 'go long ... simultaneously go short' despite already-long sequence; intended close-long/reverse-short unclear wording. ## Chapter15 — bricks reversal (PDF305–319)
- Not standard Renko: described up/down bricks produced by three higher/lower closes. Use24h intraday history, usually sub5m (examples2/3/5m), optionally daily.
- Long reversal: after directional shift count back to third brick; line across its top; buy when price crosses it. As down bricks accumulate, line trails to third brick back until crossed (305–306). Reverse for sells. Precise brick construction/overlap/height not completely algorithmically specified; do not invent exact executable detector.
- Hubert management YM: initial10point stop, sell half at+10 and stop entry−3; sell quarter of original at+20 and stop entry+3; last quarter discretionary/opposing bricks. Carter: initial20point stop, half+15, remaining on opposing brick signal, no stop trail (305,307).
- Later examples describe opposing brick followed by trailing three-brick reversal threshold for exit (309–310), rather than necessarily immediate exit on first opposing brick. Need distinguish these two formulations in research spec; exact exit unclear.
- Textual examples include large winners; no expectancy evidence. News dismissal on316 is author's opinion; not reason to disable event checks. Historical16:15ES/17:00YM hours not current verified rules.
