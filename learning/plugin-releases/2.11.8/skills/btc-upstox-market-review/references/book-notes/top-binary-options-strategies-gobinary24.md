## Payoff math and transferable education

For a simple contract with win probability `p`, net profit `W` on a win, and net loss `L` on a loss, expected value before other costs is `p × W − (1 − p) × L`. The break-even win probability is `L / (W + L)`. With a full stake loss and a 72% **net profit** on a winning stake, the break-even win rate is about 58.1%, before any additional costs or payout changes. “More wins than losses” is therefore not enough; a strategy needs a win rate above the payout-dependent threshold and robust out-of-sample evidence. Confirm whether a quoted payout is net profit or total return before calculating.

The generic teaching points that survive scrutiny are limited: define the outcome and expiry before entry; distinguish the analysis timeframe from the contract expiry; wait for price confirmation rather than predicting from a single candle; avoid treating a named pattern as certainty; account for the full loss and payout asymmetry; and test costs, payout changes, ties/refunds, execution rules and counterparty terms. These are risk-analysis questions, not buy/sell signals.
