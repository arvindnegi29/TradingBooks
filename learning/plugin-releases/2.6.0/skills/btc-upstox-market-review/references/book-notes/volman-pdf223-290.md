## Principles and qualifications

- Context outranks a recognizable trigger: trend pressure, obstruction, round-number proximity, distance from 25 EMA, pullback aggression, and adequate buildup determine whether a break is acceptable (223–225). A strong direction alone is insufficient; ranging with directional dominance remains ranging (227, 241, 251).
- Favor orderly, angular pullbacks with a clear break and room to target. Blocky opposing inventory and aggressive retracements impair continuation; however the author explicitly offsets a small block with several favorable features in Fig 8.8 (238–239). Thus these are weighted judgments, not unconditional rejection rules.
- Strong failed reversals can support continuation only after their barrier is reclaimed or sufficient obstruction is worked through. Early failure entries may occur before a technical invalidation exists (228–231). A poor long does not establish a good short (240–241).
- Buildup is relational: smaller and larger formations should have sufficient balance in time/shape. Additional bars can improve tension, yet confirmation at a higher price can worsen stop and target geometry (233, 246–247, 251).
- Entry quality includes stop survival during an ordinary retest, not merely anticipated direction. Intrabar order matters and cannot always be reconstructed from 5-minute bars (245, 270, 273, 280).
- Resistance and reversal exits are different choices. In ranges or countertrend positions, a closer obstacle may justify taking less than the nominal target; reversal exits can themselves be traps (237, 242–243, 247–249, 288).
- News-induced changes across the EMA require time to digest; neither immediate continuation nor immediate reversal follows automatically (226–227, 240–241, 286).
- Explicit epistemic limit: recurring tendencies can be anticipated, not predicted; the author warns against bias from repetition (250). The chart series is educational, with ambiguous outcomes and limited historical coverage acknowledged (255–259).

## Contrary cases and operational ambiguities

1. Fig 8.6 accepts a reversal short against a rising EMA because a substantial topping pattern changes context (234–235). Fig 8.10 accepts a long below a falling EMA because the slope reflects the pullback rather than dominant bearish pressure (243). A skill must not turn EMA side/slope into an absolute gate.
2. Fig 8.8 tolerates an initially blocky correction while Fig 8.7 rejects an orderly correction originating in a block (237–239). “Enough favorable evidence,” “harmony,” “fat,” “weak,” and “aggressive” have no reproducible thresholds here.
3. Skipped breaks can subsequently travel strongly (235); attractive-looking winners can be invalid beforehand (244–245, 288). Outcome must not retroactively label setup quality.
4. The text infers trapped traders, demoralization, buying/selling intention and double pressure from OHLC patterns. These are explanatory hypotheses, not observed participant identities, positions, or order-flow measurements (224–251).
5. Statements that good/bad fortune balances over time (249, 258) do not prove expectancy. The compounding illustration assumes steady returns, 2% trade risk and sustained weekly consistency (258–259); it is arithmetic conditional on assumptions, not supported achievable performance.
6. “High odds” is qualitative. No denominators, execution records, spread/commission accounting, sample-independent rules, confidence intervals or out-of-sample results are provided in this segment. Six months of sequential charts reduces one obvious selection concern but is not a mechanical backtest (255–259).

## Transfer to BTC and Indian markets

General observation vocabulary can transfer as hypotheses: trend versus range, obstruction, retests, failed breaks, volatility shifts, and stop geometry. The author's universal applicability statement is itself qualified by instrument and timezone characteristics (256). EUR/USD 2012 five-minute settings do not validate BTC perpetuals, BTC options, Indian index futures/options or equities. Do not copy 20/10-pip brackets, 00/50 round levels, CET liquidity windows or the 25-EMA configuration as proven parameters. BTC trades continuously with venue-specific spreads, funding, liquidation dynamics and weekend liquidity; Indian instruments have session boundaries, opening gaps and distinct expiries. Options additionally depend on spread, depth, volatility and Greeks, so underlying direction does not establish option trade expectancy. These are transfer limitations identified by review, not claims that this source tested those markets. A future implementation needs explicit, predeclared definitions and fresh venue/instrument execution constraints before evaluation.
