## Findings usable as conditional teaching principles

- Pattern names alone do not authorize entries. Trend dominance, buildup, distance from the EMA, nearby prior barriers, round-number placement, and session timing jointly qualify or disqualify the same apparent pattern (PDF 295, 304, 308, 323–325, 334–335, 356–358).
- Trading a break for failure is preferentially aligned with the larger prevailing bias and a first meaningful EMA pullback. Later pullbacks or fading a strong same-direction market are weaker candidates (PDF 296, 304, 316, 328, 347, 357). This is a preference, not a mechanically defined exclusion.
- Round levels can be destinations, obstacles, or sources of mean-reversion risk. A level cannot be classified merely by proximity: the side of the entry, prior defense, trend strength, and room to target matter (PDF 298–300, 308–310, 323–324, 337, 341–344, 355–358).
- Waiting is a substantive action: unclear dominance, thin or aimless ranges, tall erratic bars, weak squeezes, and scheduled event uncertainty repeatedly lead to skipped trades (PDF 299, 305–306, 308, 317–318, 325, 331, 340–342, 345–346, 356, 358).
- Exit management is discretionary and can require re-entry. A reversal exit may save risk or expel a valid trade; a resistance exit can be appropriate on a slow approach to a round target (PDF 309, 317, 320, 336, 349–351). The author explicitly leaves the choice unresolved in Fig 9.77b.
- Scheduled news risk can override a technically valid setup; follow-up speeches can matter even if the initial decision seems uneventful (PDF 305–306, 329, 332, 350–351, 357–358).

## Contrary cases and ambiguities that must remain explicit

1. **News continuation is a strong caution, not an absolute empirical law.** PDF 301, 305, 347, 357 advise against continuation; PDF 326 (Fig 9.67c) nonetheless describes an unusually well-developed post-news opportunity. Any skill phrased as universal prohibition would erase the exception; any skill phrased as profitable permission would overstate the evidence.
2. **EMA contact is contextual.** PDF 324 rejects a shallow pullback that has not reached the EMA, but PDF 332 accepts a pullback that misses the extended pattern line after a substantial swing retracement; PDF 345 rejects an EMA pullback while the prior selloff is still being digested. A touch rule is not the method.
3. **First pullback and dominant trend do not settle management.** PDF 320 gives a first-pullback exit exception due to major left-side resistance. PDF 313 allows aggressive entries because of exceptional bearish strength; PDF 325 rejects bullish participation despite pressure because the bars are too tall.
4. **Winning retrospectively does not validate entry quality.** PDF 337 explicitly rejects shorts that look attractive after the move. PDF 348 Fig 9.89c marks a long unfavorable even though the later chart rallies strongly. Outcome and ex-ante quality must remain separate.
5. **No stable quantitative boundaries supplied here.** Terms such as strong, thin, aggressive, sufficient buildup, dominant, absorption and demoralization lack operational thresholds. EMA period is explicit (25), but execution policy, signal confirmation, stop buffer and target adaptation cannot be uniquely derived from these annotations.
6. **Mental-state explanations are interpretations.** Repeated failed attacks and failed W/M patterns are observable; claims that participants capitulate, become demoralized, anticipate a report, or pursue a magnet infer psychology from price (PDF 308, 348, 353, 355, 358). They are hypotheses, not separately observed causation.
7. **Intrabar order matters.** PDF 348 Fig 9.89a says the quality would differ if adverse tests occurred before the signal-bar break. A five-minute OHLC candle cannot always identify that ordering. PDF 353 offers pattern-line break confirmation as an alternative to the earlier signal-bar trigger, creating different executable strategies.

## Selected chart inspection

Rendered and visually inspected all three panels on physical PDF 336 (printed 326; Fig 9.77a–c), 348 (printed 338; Fig 9.89a–c), and 353 (printed 343; Fig 9.94a–c), at 1800-pixel page height.

- PDF 336: thin overnight action and a false-break trap precede later short management; numbered bars 1/2 make the early-versus-late reversal exit dilemma concrete. Later adverse recovery reinforces that there is no single illustrated mandatory exit.
- PDF 348: top panel combines tall signal bar with three adverse tests; middle panel connects failed W recovery attempts to a bearish hypothesis while requiring monitoring of the range floor. Bottom panel warns against an early/unfavorable long despite the subsequent rise. Strong evidence against outcome-based relabeling.
- PDF 353: small signal-bar trigger lies before a descending pattern-line breach; waiting for that breach changes the entry. Middle panel marks premature continuation and a later false-high failure; bottom panel ties a reversal candidate to a sizable retracement and subsequently skips late shorts. These are conditional sequence examples, not sufficient statistics.

## BTC and India transfer limits

These pages supply historical spot-FX chart examples, not validation on BTC perpetuals, BTC options, Indian indices, equities, or index options. Preserve their questions about structure, obstacles, timing and invalidation; do not transfer EUR/USD pip targets, 00/50 spacing, CET release/open timestamps, or session liquidity assumptions directly. BTC runs continuously and instrument/venue liquidity can vary; Indian market sessions and event schedules require current local calendars. Derivatives add spreads, fees, funding or expiry effects, leverage and (for options) volatility/Greek exposure: an underlying chart signal does not specify an option contract or executable premium outcome. Any operational implementation requires fresh instrument-specific data, liquidity checks, timestamp normalization and explicit entry/exit conventions before testing. No backtest or performance estimate was performed or established by this review.
