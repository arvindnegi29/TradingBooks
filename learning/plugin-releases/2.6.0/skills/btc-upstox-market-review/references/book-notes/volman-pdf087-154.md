## Principles supported by this section

- Context precedes entry pattern. First assess dominant pressure, market pace, buildup, adverse magnets behind entry and obstructions toward target. The moving average, arches and failed attempts provide context; no isolated candlestick or pattern supplies a sufficient signal (physical 96–98, 118–119, 122–123).
- Standard pattern-break execution waits for a completed signal bar and entry beyond its extreme. The source specifies one EUR/USD pip, favors a bar closing with the intended direction, allows tiny opposite bodies/dojis, and rejects entry below a strongly bullish bar or above a strongly bearish bar (90–91). These are the author's execution conventions, not universal exchange rules.
- A tightening final arch, repeated failures to escape, and a signal bar near the barrier help justify a tight stop. Greater barrier significance generally calls for more buildup; there is explicitly no fixed minimum bar count (93–99, 104–106).
- Stop geometry matters: a signal sticking far outside the pattern may leave insufficient room to test the boundary; the preferred stop remains within the old pattern (99). Dominant direction alone does not rescue an entry far from support, the average or a round number (104–105, 113–115).
- A missed or unacceptable first breakout can later offer a pattern-break pullback: orderly return to the broken barrier, a small consolidation outside it, or stalling across the barrier itself (107–117). A new valid signal is needed; a missed trade should not trigger pursuit or an attempt to repair the miss (131–133).
- Combi means powerbar plus inside bar, with the latter usually serving as signal. Reverse order, two dojis, extra inside bars, tiny opposite bodies and slightly protruding bars can qualify in context. Shared extremes are helpful rather than mandatory; the pattern is timing machinery, not standalone directional evidence (118–119, 125–127, 133).
- Pullback reversal distinguishes correction in time from correction in price. Approximate 50–60% retracement, or 40% in a strong trend, is guidance; an average touch plus technical test is preferred. Orderliness and bar size relative to the preceding swing matter; a forceful counter-move requires more proof of exhaustion (111–112, 135–139).
- Distinguish a trend pullback from a short-term swing already at the edge of a larger range. The same entry shape can have different continuation prospects because the larger range attracts opposing orders (140–143).
- Treat news separately from chart structure. The source explicitly identifies slippage, adverse spikes and inability to guarantee bracket loss at release time (134–135, 154). Bracket orders are a management convention; the author makes no superiority claim and introduces discretionary exits for news, near-target resistance and reversal developments (153–154).

## Contrary cases and boundaries that must survive any skill condensation

- Figure 5.2 gives a plausible long that nearly reaches target and then challenges its stop; a sound-looking setup does not guarantee an outcome (92–95). Do not move a stop away to evade a loss (94).
- Figure 5.4 contains an acknowledged lucky target outcome and an explicit reminder that individual chart outcomes are educational. Its later bull flag is accepted despite reluctance about the untested round number and lunch period (100–103). The rendered chart confirms both the short-lived downside move and later lengthy flag; the exception is visible, not a separate quantitative rule.
- Figure 5.5 allows entry almost on, rather than beyond, the pattern line because the pattern is deemed mature, while Figure 5.3 advocates one more bar (98 versus 106). A rigid rule of always waiting one extra bar contradicts the source.
- Figure 5.7 contrasts a gentle pullback with a full, powerful retracement; one additional inside bar can make the latter more acceptable (110–112). No effect size is measured.
- Figure 5.8 accepts an early valid entry even though a later one looks better retrospectively; one cannot demand future confirmation unavailable at first entry (114–115).
- Stale sideways action must not be equated automatically with meaningful buildup (120). The text infers low volume from price appearance, without furnishing volume observations.
- Figure 5.14 permits an aggressive exception despite a poor pattern line and no average test, and states conservative traders may skip (128–130). It cannot support a categorical mandate to trade or avoid that geometry.
- Figure 5.15 explains that a post-breakout cluster can invalidate an immediate replacement entry and potentially justify scratching an existing position, complicating simplistic instructions to hold every pullback (131–135).
- Figure 5.18 explicitly accepts the first mature flag without an average touch, then rejects later smaller flags far from the average (144–147). Visual inspection confirms the first flag's substantial duration relative to later small flags. The EMA-touch filter is a default with discretionary exceptions, not a necessity.
- Figure 5.19 allows trades from within a range; the upper barrier may act as obstacle or magnet depending on buildup. Equal entry price does not imply equal setup quality (148–151).

## Operational and causal ambiguities

- Terms including dominance, mature/ripeness, sufficient buildup, thick/thin, strong pullback and favorable odds are discretionary. They require explicit operational definitions before repeatable scanning or historical testing. Do not invent numerical thresholds and attribute them to this source.
- Pattern lines are redrawn and spikes disregarded according to later touches (89–90, 97, 105, 117). A systematic implementation must fix when lines become eligible and prohibit hindsight revisions to earlier trade decisions.
- Narratives about fear, morale, trapped traders, capitulation and double pressure are plausible hypotheses inferred from OHLC paths; these pages do not observe trader intent, order books or causal flows. Label them interpretations rather than verified mechanisms.
- OHLC cannot recover intrabar order. The author explicitly acknowledges uncertainty whether the false low preceded the breakout in Figure 5.2 (93–94). Both stop/target collision and exact fills require finer data, spread and execution assumptions.
- One winner offsetting two losses at 2:1 assumes exact gross fills and excludes fees/slippage; its 1/3 breakeven statement is not a measured win rate (109–110, 135). Costs increase the required rate.
- Chart-angle judgments depend on axis proportions; the source advises consistent scaling while conceding no hard angle rule (116–117). Use scale-independent definitions if automating.
- Strong language such as nine-times-out-of-ten, high odds and precise anticipation lacks a reported sample, denominator or controlled study in these pages (95, 126, 141, 148). Preserve as author's opinion, not empirical probability.
- The EMA description at physical 144 loosely describes 25 bars; implement a true exponential recursion rather than a 25-bar simple mean or cutoff.

## BTC and Indian-market transfer limits

These pages illustrate EUR/USD five-minute charts, round numbers in 50/100-pip increments, a 10-pip stop and 20-pip target, and European/US session rhythms. They do not validate BTC perpetuals/options or Indian cash/index options. Any adoption should keep the structure vocabulary as hypotheses and independently define instrument ticks, volatility-scaled risk, meaningful round levels, session calendar/timezone/DST, liquidity and spread filters. BTC's continuous trading, funding and liquidation mechanics differ from spot FX; Indian sessions, opening gaps and scheduled events also differ. Options premium includes volatility, time decay and nonlinear exposure, so an underlying chart signal cannot specify option entry risk or fill quality. Verify current venue contract sizes, expiry, order types, fees and market data rather than copying the pip bracket. No trading recommendation, backtest or plugin change was produced here.
