## Original analytical notes
- PDF13–14: study and personal methodology precede live risk. Adopting third-party methods as shortcuts is discouraged; education is preparation, not a promise of profitability.
- PDF15–18: seven principles frame the method: double pressure, support/resistance, three breakout qualities, false highs/lows, pullback reversal, ceiling test and round numbers. Double pressure interprets new directional participation plus forced opposite exits. It is a proposed mechanism, not directly measured participant flow.
- PDF19–21: support/resistance are context. Observe behavior around levels before trading either break or bounce. Slowing momentum or an initial reversal attempt need not terminate dominance; a failed recovery after a break is stronger contextual evidence than the first isolated breach.
- PDF21–26: poor/no buildup, tease/buildup away from barrier, proper/buildup at barrier are classifications made from information before entry. Winning does not make a poor setup good; losing does not make a well-set break poor. Assess dominance, trend/range, obstacles, buildup and defensible stop together. A tease can rectify later, but need not.
- PDF26–30: false pattern break differs from single-bar false high/low. A rejection against the trend, a failed corrective extension and failure after well-built continuation have different implications. A false high/low alone does not authorize an entry; follow-on buildup and trigger matter.
- PDF30–35: 40–60% retracement and technical tests are context, not automatic limit entries. Wait for turning evidence/buildup; this may miss trades. A later entry with a tighter valid stop can have similar reward/risk to an earlier cheaper entry. Ratios alone do not establish expectancy.
- PDF35–39: ceiling test includes floors in the author's terminology; identify last internal support/resistance in an arch, not just the outermost range boundary. Its possible retest can threaten tight stops; a second break after such a test may have different credentials. The magnet/bounce explanation remains conditional.
- PDF40–42: historical EUR/USD 00/50 levels and lower-volatility20-levels are author conventions. A round level may be helpful destination or adverse return magnet; direction, location and history determine role. Do not transplant pip spacing to BTC/India.
- PDF43–45: historical study cannot reproduce live experience. The25EMA is an optional orientation/filter, not an independent edge. The source loosely describes an exponential average as last25closes; calculate an actual causal EMA recursion with disclosed seed/warmup, not a finite SMA25. Historical CET/session times need current calendar/DST verification.
- PDF46–59: chart examples combine established boundary, technical test, orderly correction, false high/low and buildup near the break. Flat/descending resistance lines for longs and flat/ascending support lines for shorts are source conventions. Boxes and lines may be adjusted as new bars arrive; preserve their decision-time state and never rewrite old entries with future touches. EMA-pattern squeeze differs from Carter's volatility squeeze.
- PDF60–69: no-buildup breaks far from the average or after an extended move can be poor despite trend alignment. Candle shape alone does not establish reversal. EMA itself is an average, not physical support/resistance (PDF61). Relative flag/pole time and size (harmony) influence judgment. Countertrend W/M breaks may justify exit without authorizing new opposite entry.
- PDF70–75: failed perforation can leave earlier boundary more relevant, subject to later observed alignment. Inside bars and powerbars require location/context. Author's triple combines EMA, technical test and round level or retracement; these are overlapping price-derived observations, not three independent probabilities. Strong versus weak doubles distinguishes the presence of a technical test.
- PDF77–81: historical20pip target/10pip stop and manual-market-entry/OCO model are examples, not imported defaults. Source acknowledges slippage and nonfilled limit protection risk. Correct the categorical implication that market stops always exit: order availability, rejection, gaps, outages, insufficient liquidity and fill-price uncertainty remain; book text cannot guarantee execution or broker order types. Adapt proposed risk distance to structure/volatility and round quantity down to the user's budget; do not widen an existing trade's risk casually. Exact gross20/10 gives1/3breakeven only before friction unless distances are demonstrably net; include actual spread, commissions and slippage separately.
- PDF83–86: context/dominance plus buildup plus actual signal. Completed signal bar differs from subsequent entry bar: the author's entry can occur intrabar when a prior completed signal extreme is exceeded, not only at entry-bar close. No partial signal-bar confirmation. Full-pip rounding is historical chart convention, not permission to alter exchange ticks or execution feed.

## Implementation limits
Dominance, mature buildup, meaningful barrier, harmony, stretched and acceptable obstacles lack complete numerical definitions. Do not claim automated detection without documented causal definitions and calculations. OHLC cannot uniquely show the path or prove trapped traders' intentions. Price-derived confluence is correlated. Every operational trigger needs decision/known-at time, frozen boundary, relevant completed context, actual fresh execution quote, tick buffer and risk/cancellation conditions. None of these pages tests BTC or Indian option premium returns; retain exact contract, current fees/funding/Greeks, liquidity and manual-execution gates.
