## Principles suitable for source-grounded knowledge

- News risk is both instrument-specific and execution-specific. Assess relevant calendars and historical responses, including daylight-saving alignment; a stop-trigger price need not be the fill. The hypothetical symmetric spike arithmetic is explicitly rejected once slippage, spread and platform response are considered (155–157). The recommended default is avoidance, with situational judgment. The historical EUR/USD clock times and broker observations are not current universal facts.
- Resistance exits are proactive, planned before entry; reversal exits react to subsequent adverse structure (158–159, 165–167, 222). Planning the obstruction level helps prevent fear-based target shrinking. Prefer full targets where plausible; a trend can clear barriers that would justify caution in a range (159–164).
- The resistance-exit example uses roughly 14 pip minimum reward against a 10 pip stop, with 10 pip as the author's lower suggested limit (158). These are examples within a EUR/USD bracket convention, not general risk sizing rules or a demonstrated optimal reward/risk threshold.
- Do not reflexively scratch the first pullback. A normal diagonal retest can attract aligned participation; a mature sideways cluster with a strong adverse powerbar/combi and a break is more concerning (166–173, 184–185). Evaluate reversal buildup relative to its preceding impulse, not by pattern name alone (169).
- An exit signal need not be a valid opposite entry. This is explicit for evening/morning-star variants and the large reversal range against an earlier trend (179, 183–185). A position's survival threshold differs from the threshold for initiating new risk.
- Trade selection requires pressure, surrounding conditions and setup together (187–189, 221–222). Skips include trend opposition, nearby obstruction, range mistaken for trend, excessive EMA distance, adverse round-number geometry, frantic bar ranges, aggressive corrections and insufficient buildup (189).
- Trade-for-failure is conditional, not automatic fading: identify a counterdominant break, observe failure, and enter only once the trigger is back across the broken boundary (188, 190–193, 207–208, 216). On a failed downside break the long trigger must be at/above the original barrier; the converse applies to a failed upside break (191, 207).
- First countertrend attempts in a fresh dominant swing are preferred failure candidates. Later, larger reversal structures become harder to dismantle; subsequent failures may be optional rather than equal-quality repeats (196, 217). A mature acceleration can warn against another continuation entry, but is expressly a weak standalone reversal forecast (192, 201–202).
- Ww/Mm describes a base plus a smaller squeeze, with an agreed boundary and proportionate buildup. The source discourages a rigid bar-count rule and treats premature teases as potentially rectified by later structure (210–213).
- Extraordinary trend strength does not guarantee an executable tight-stop entry. Strong moves lacking intermediate technical reference points can leave entry consensus divided; abstention remains valid (217–219).

## Contrary cases and important qualifications

- Resistance can be overcome by sufficiently strong buildup, even in a range; an early entry and later pullback entry can therefore warrant different exits (163–165).
- The usual anti-scratch rule has explicit exceptions: an unusually forceful adverse combi immediately after breakout may justify an early reversal exit even during the first correction (176–180). Figure 6.7 supplies the counterexample: the first weak reversal bar in a strong bullish setting does not warrant the same response as the later stronger M formation (182–183).
- Poor entries may still win. The author states most skipped entries in the volatile Figure 7.4 session would have reached 20 pip (203). Outcome alone does not validate the entry filter; conversely, this is not evidence that the filter improves expectancy.
- A news-disrupted flag can look textbook yet be poorly located relative to EMA and untested round number (174–175). A visually attractive correction after a sharp reversal can also be poor continuation because of the origin of the impulse and distant support (180–181, 204–209).
- The slope filter is qualified: a lengthy angular correction can tilt the EMA against the intended resumption, and is treated differently from directly opposing a dominant trending average (216). Thus a literal always-follow-EMA rule would misstate the source.
- A failed adverse break is insufficient by itself. Missing boundary recapture, a weak trigger, overhead structure, later trend stage or adverse target geometry can still rule out the trade (193, 196, 213, 216–217).
- Round levels have two-sided roles: attraction before arrival, possible reversal/obstruction on arrival, and dangerous stop placement when entry sits just beyond them (168–169, 179–181, 197, 209). The source does not assert that touching a round level guarantees either continuation or reversal.

## Operational and causal ambiguities

- Terms such as strong/trending, harmonious, substantial buildup, fresh/mature trend, orderly correction, enough squeeze, reasonable obstruction and favorable conditions have no fixed numerical definitions here. Powerbars are relative to nearby activity; some signals are single-bar, others combined. Converting this prose into a deterministic scanner requires explicit additional assumptions and validation, not quotation as an algorithm.
- Boundary placement can change as later bars develop (212), and multiple reasonable box ceilings exist (216). For live or historical testing, preserve the boundary known at the decision time and report alternative plausible boundaries; otherwise recapture/failure labels can acquire hindsight bias.
- Accounts of trapped traders, contrarian designs, demoralization, consensus, profit taking and sideline participation are explanatory hypotheses inferred from price bars. They do not establish actual participant identities, intentions or causal order flow. Page 173 expressly says whether a false low was engineered is irrelevant to the observable implication; page 191 admits uncertainty about traders' thinking.
- Some text infers intrabar path from completed candle shape (176–177). OHLC generally cannot uniquely recover that path; a faithful implementation must require actual lower-timeframe/tick evidence or treat the narrative as illustrative. Printed page 167's description of the initial bullish breakout as a 'right leg up' also appears terminologically inconsistent with the earlier left-leg convention; preserve the observed structure rather than codifying that label.
- Advice to compare actual manual scratches with untouched outcomes (159) is a useful research question, not a completed comparison. Similarly a year of selected high-quality examples is suggested at 203; retrospective discretionary selection still risks selection bias unless predeclared and reproducible.
- Qualifiers like high odds, strongest follow-through or nine-in-ten preference are author judgments here, without a displayed statistical design or sample denominator (190, 208, 210). They must not become model confidence percentages.
- The 20/10 bracket is explicitly non-optimal and illustrative even for 5-minute EUR/USD (222). Ignoring spreads, fees, slippage and ambiguous within-bar target/stop ordering would not be a source-supported performance calculation.

## Transfer limits for BTC and Indian markets (reviewer's interpretation)

- Retain structural hypotheses and decision distinctions; do not transplant pip targets, EUR/USD round-number spacing, EMA distance tolerances, session clock times or lunch effects. Test each instrument and venue with locally appropriate spread, tick size, volatility and liquidity measurements (source basis: 155–157, 189, 201, 206, 210, 222).
- BTC's continuous trading means the forex weekend-close instruction (157) does not map literally; weekend liquidity and event exposure require independent treatment. India cash/index derivatives have their own sessions, holiday gaps and event calendar. These differences are transfer cautions, not facts tested in this PDF review.
- Underlying price-action hypotheses do not directly determine an option premium entry/stop. Expiry, delta/gamma, volatility, spread and liquidity change the translation; nor does a futures chart capture leverage liquidation, funding or venue-specific execution. Those mechanics need separate current data and specifications.
- Neither BTC nor Indian-market predictive performance is established by this source. Any production knowledge should label these as discretionary chart-reading concepts and require fresh instrument-specific evidence for execution or numeric plans.
