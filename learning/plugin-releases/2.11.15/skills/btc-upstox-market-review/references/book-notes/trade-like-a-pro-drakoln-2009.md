## Chart-reading, marking and scan workflow supported by this source

Use this as a source-attributed checklist, not a standalone signal model:

1. Identify the instrument, venue, chart timeframe and price scale. Keep the “before” chart separate from the after-the-fact outcome.
2. Classify the visible larger move as trend, counter-trend or sideways/transition. The 50-day moving average is the book's historical regime aid; use it only as context, not a universal threshold.
3. Mark horizontal support and resistance from visible prior swing points and repeated reactions. Add a trendline only when its swing anchors are visible; label the anchors and timeframe. Examples include support/resistance around the S&P 1525 area (printed pp. 244–245), crude-oil support and descending resistance (pp. 159–160), and the euro collar levels (pp. 114–117).
4. Describe a chart shape as a candidate: triple top/head-and-shoulders, triangle/flag, gap, or range. State the observed boundaries and what would negate the reading. The author uses candlestick names and 50-day MA context in places, but does not define a reproducible pattern detector.
5. Before discussing a trade, record entry, invalidation/loss level, target/profit level, trend case and counter-trend case (Chapter 3, pp. 56–63). Compare the distance to the next opposing level with actual instrument costs and risk. A visible support/resistance line is a test area, not a guaranteed reversal.
6. Scan a manageable set of markets and setups instead of chasing alerts across unrelated instruments (Chapter 3, pp. 53–56). For each candidate, preserve source/timeframe for every marked level and distinguish observed levels from projected targets.

The examples also display the 50-day moving average, RSI and Bollinger bands. Treat these as optional, potentially correlated context. The book's examples do not establish that a crossover, candlestick, triangle, support touch or indicator reading predicts the next move. Several figure pages are raw charts with the proposed interpretation described in the prose; chart labels are sparse and inconsistent, so this book does not supply a comprehensive label taxonomy, algorithmic scan, SMC/ICT method, order-flow method or institutional-participant identification rule.

## Use in BTC and Indian-market reviews

For Delta BTCUSD or an Upstox Indian instrument, use only the chart process above after validating fresh candles, venue, session and contract specifications. Mark current levels from current data; never reuse the 2007–2008 U.S. futures prices, the book's example option premiums, contract multipliers, margin figures, “one-for-two” heuristics or U.S. exchange assumptions. BTC is continuous and Indian markets have distinct sessions and derivatives specifications; state those differences where they affect the chart.

The source's most transferable contribution is planning the loss/invalidation and target beside the entry, considering a contrary trend/range case, and assessing the whole position rather than a single leg. Option structures are educational here: verify current chain liquidity, Greeks, expiry/settlement, lot size, spread, fees, slippage, funding/carry and margin before discussing a conditional scenario. Do not recommend naked option selling, assume listed BTC options exist on the user's venue, or count a hedge/spread as loss-free. Preserve the plugin's user risk budget, WAIT/NO TRADE gates and manual-only boundary.

This is a complete-book knowledge review, not empirical replication. Chapter examples are hindsight/hypothetical, some annualized short-option returns extrapolate a short sample, market claims are dated, and the source's trading tools are largely U.S. futures/options. No BTC/India strategy, detector, parameter or profitability edge has been validated.
