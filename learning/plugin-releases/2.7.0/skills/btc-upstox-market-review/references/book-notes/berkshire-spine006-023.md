# Raw-source evaluation: spine 006–023 (1986–1994)

All assigned extracted text was read completely in four bounded, untruncated batches: 006–011, 012–017, 018–021, 022–023. These are historical meeting/newsletter notes, not a verbatim transcript or present market research. Page citations are unavailable; references below use EPUB href and named section.

## Principles with transferable analytical use

- **1986, Value Investing / Intrinsic Business Value / Economic Forecasting** (`index_split_005.html`): distinguish business value from quoted price and allow a substantial cushion for valuation error. Buffett is reported to focus on intrinsic value rather than forecasts. The opening Graham framework, superiority claims, and recommendation language are the narrator’s exposition; they should not be represented as direct Buffett quotations or validation of a trading system.
- **1987, Know Your Limitations / False Precision** (`index_split_007.html`): Buffett/Munger emphasize competence, humility, and limitations of information and computer outputs. Transferable safeguard: expose uncertainty, avoid precise probabilities unsupported by evidence, and decline conclusions outside available data. The chapter also includes Templeton, Michaelis, and Wesco material; collective observations are not all Berkshire-meeting statements.
- **1988, Inflation Hedges / Predicting Business Cycles / Successor Businesses** (`index_split_009.html`): leverage can prevent survival; extrapolating a macro thesis into speculative hedges is not endorsed. Durable business quality matters more than guessing cycles. Seeing technological displacement does not establish that investing in the replacement industry is attractive.
- **1989, IBV / Past Is Not Necessarily Prologue / Junk Bonds** (`index_split_011.html`): value depends on uncertain future net cash flows, and historical returns do not establish future safety. Funding structures that defer cash payment can hide inability to pay; incentives to pass exposure onward merit scrutiny. The narrator’s ServiceMaster implied valuation is explicitly an inference, not Buffett’s disclosed target.
- **1990, Buy Wonderful Businesses** (`index_split_013.html`): earnings power and capital returns may matter more than liquidation discounts. Michaelis supplies the assets-versus-earnings-power distinction; Munger’s remarks come partly from Wesco. The shift qualifies a simplistic rule that every cheap asset is desirable.
- **1991, Permanent Holdings / Financial World / Looking for Seven-Footers / Media Business** (`index_split_015.html`): favorable economics, trustworthy management and documented behavior support long ownership; financial-company incentives can defer pain while risk accumulates. Secular deterioration can weaken previously excellent businesses, requiring reassessment. Coca-Cola operating statistics are partly Don Keough’s presentation, and narrator footnotes favoring Berkshire are not independent investment evidence.
- **1992, All Investing Is Value Investing / Equity Coupon / Market Timing** (`index_split_017.html`): growth can destroy value when incremental capital earns poorly; book value is an input record rather than a universal valuation proxy. Accounting and compensation adjustments affect apparent returns. If cash flows cannot be estimated reasonably, abstention is appropriate. The narrator’s Guinness valuation and intentional-globalization interpretation are marked inferences, not disclosed Buffett calculations.
- **1993, Value Investing / Generics vs. Brands / Key to Successful Investing** (`index_split_019.html`): select situations where there is an explainable edge; great historical statistics do not establish current business quality. Brands vary in defensibility, pricing gap and distribution strength. Competence requires knowing its boundary. Munger’s concentrated lifelong opportunity examples are not a universal position-sizing instruction.
- **1994, Super Catastrophes / Reinsurance / Evaluating Management / What vs. When / Think for Yourself** (`index_split_021.html`): assess exposure and plausible severe outcomes rather than loss history alone; avoid pressure to transact when pricing is unattractive. Evaluate management against peers, capital allocation and starting conditions. Independent judgment and source quality matter more than information speed for this stated long-term approach.

## Contrary cases and limits that must remain visible

1. **Derivatives are not treated uniformly.** In 1988 Program Trading (`index_split_009.html`), Munger strongly criticizes index options and Buffett uses an island/futures analogy, while accepting arbitrage’s market function. In 1993 Danger in Derivatives (`index_split_019.html`), intertwined obligations create systemic risks that Salomon cannot fully hedge. In 1994 Derivatives (`index_split_021.html`), Buffett explicitly recognizes useful purposes and emphasizes ignorance combined with borrowing. This supports understanding exposure, counterparties and leverage; it supports neither a blanket claim that every derivative is bad nor an endorsed options-selling technique.
2. **Macro views are historical opinions, not timing signals.** Repeated 1986–1988 inflation concerns coexist with statements that strategy would not change; 1993 Inflation Will Return admits inflation had stayed unexpectedly low. 1987’s 8–12-year municipal bonds qualify simplistic avoidance of all bonds. Record both view and timing uncertainty; do not carry old rates, prices, trade-deficit figures or expectations into current analysis.
3. **Long holding periods do not excuse changing fundamentals.** The 1988 confidence in encyclopedias’ stability is a historical expectation, not a verified enduring fact. The 1991 downgrade of media economics and 1993 brand distinctions show that franchise quality can weaken. The narrator’s 1988 Salomon core-holding suggestion precedes 1992’s account of its Treasury scandal; keep the suggestion, speaker and subsequent problem distinct.
4. **Risk is not only volatility, but volatility still matters to margined instruments.** 1994 Modern Portfolio Theory (`index_split_021.html`) concerns economic loss, insurance exposure, probabilities and a long time horizon. It cannot justify ignoring mark-to-market, liquidation, premium loss, expiry, gaps or inability to finance a position in intraday/derivatives trading. Lumpy insurance examples are not evidence for retail options return targets.
5. **No demonstrated intraday edge.** Business cash-flow valuation, annual reports, permanent ownership, and 1994’s tolerance for delayed quotes are incompatible with treating this material as validation of fast entries, technical indicators, Greeks thresholds or expiry selection. Transfer only general reasoning safeguards; any trading rules require separate instrument-specific evidence and current executable market data.
6. **No mechanical numerical transplant.** 1989’s reported broad valuation range and deep purchase discount, 1993’s government-bond discount-rate convention, and historical ROE figures illustrate judgment in context. They do not establish universal safety multiples, a required discount rate, or measured probabilities. The book’s historical performance tables and enthusiastic narrator claims do not establish future profitability or generalizability.
