# Source-grounding review: spine 024–039 (1995–2002)

Scope: Every character of the sixteen extracted spine texts was read in bounded, untruncated batches (024–027, 028–031, 032–035, 036–037, 038–039). This is an evaluation of historical source support, not current market advice or a validation of trading performance. The book presents authors' edited meeting notes, narration, footnotes, and occasional articles; reported Buffett/Munger remarks are not a verbatim independently verified transcript.

## Principles and boundaries

- **1995, Stocks / Accounting / On Projections** (`index_split_023.html`): Reported Buffett rejects anchoring to one's purchase price; reported Munger distinguishes cutting a gambling loss from trying to recover it the same way. Legitimate transfer: assess the next decision on its merits, scrutinize confusing accounts, and challenge forecasts produced to justify a desired outcome. This does not establish stop-loss distances, revenge-trading thresholds, or an execution model.
- **1995, Business Castles / Intrinsic Business Value** (`index_split_023.html`): Durable competitive advantages, management integrity and intrinsic value are business-owner concepts. The authors explain the framework and attribute specific examples to Buffett/Munger. Newspapers being attractive monopolies is a 1995 assessment, not a timeless industry rule; later technological-change sections qualify permanence.
- **1995, Derivatives** (`index_split_023.html`): Reported Buffett emphasizes signature-created leverage and risk creation; Munger condemns option exchanges. This is contrary evidence against presenting an options-trading method as their endorsed strategy. It supports evaluating leverage and hidden exposures, not an empirically calibrated derivatives ban or permission rule.
- **1996, Intrinsic Business Value / Buybacks / Float** (`index_split_025.html`): Buffett's reported cash-flow valuation and buybacks below intrinsic value are conditional, not price/book shortcuts. Float is valuable only with sound underwriting; costs remain uncertain until claims settle. Corporate insurance float is not interchangeable with retail margin, option premium, or broker financing.
- **1996, Diversification / Wonderful Companies** (`index_split_025.html`): Concentration advocacy is expressly tied to knowing the businesses; their inability to forecast technological change leads them to decline opportunities. Do not convert concentration anecdotes into a universal position-sizing rule or claim that competence in reading charts equals business-underwriting competence.
- **1997, Irrational Exuberance / Business Risk / Market Risk / Filters** (`index_split_027.html`): Valuation matters even for excellent businesses; debt, capital intensity and commodity economics are distinct risks. Opportunity cost, understandable economics, and quality people filter choices. Volatility being useful to long-horizon investors does not mean volatility is harmless for leveraged or expiring positions. Dow levels, interest rates, return expectations and specific competitive claims belong to 1997.
- **1997, Insurance / On Learning** (`index_split_027.html`): A known dollar exposure is contextualized against Berkshire liquid assets, while unexpected aggregation can destroy insurers. This supports explicit aggregate exposure and survival analysis, not copying the example's 2.5% loss ratio as a trading limit. Learning from prior mistakes and See's-to-Coke pattern recognition is supported; pattern recognition alone is not demonstrated intraday predictive power.
- **1998, Mega Merger / Buffett Is Selling Stocks / Cash Is King** (`index_split_029.html`): Crucial attribution boundary: 1998 has no meeting newsletter; these are the authors' July/August articles. The General Re merger is introduced through a press release; adjusted-equity estimates, interpretation of stock dilution as selling, inferred interest-rate views, cash-as-signal reasoning and a possible market downdraft are authors' analysis/speculation. Do not quote those inferences as Buffett's market-timing instructions. The merger-discount example is a historical conditional deal trade, not a current opportunity or proof of safe arbitrage.
- **1999, The Market / Future Returns / Tough to Predict / Business Moat** (`index_split_031.html`): Reported Buffett says they seek businesses rather than think about the overall market; cash accumulates when opportunities are absent. This qualifies the authors' 1998 timing interpretation. Distinguish favorable industry growth from owners earning profits, and reassess moats when distribution technology changes. Contemporary Internet and furniture/jewelry predictions cannot be treated as present facts.
- **2000, Technology Stocks / Big Truth / Effective Rationality** (`index_split_033.html`): Explicit criticism of speculative day trading and performance chasing is contrary evidence for attributing an intraday system to these speakers. Munger's model plurality supports checking multiple explanations, not piling on unvalidated indicators. Some remarks are from the separate Wesco meeting, clearly identified in the source; do not label them Berkshire-meeting quotations.
- **2001, Biggest Mistake / Extrapolating the Past / Berkshire Advantage** (`index_split_035.html`): Missed opportunities count as mistakes only within competence; missing cocoa futures does not. Avoid forcing trades from fear of missing out. Historical compounding and optimistic growth projections are challenged. Berkshire's cash certainty, stable ownership and insurance operations are special institutional advantages, not replicable retail assumptions.
- **2001, Know the Big Cost / Labor Costs / California Utility Crisis** (`index_split_035.html`): Understand the economic cost driver and maintain spare capacity; safety analogies support buffers qualitatively, not a particular margin percentage. Commodity versus service differentiation matters. Sugar/product-liability assurances and utility-policy judgments are historical reported opinions, not medical/legal facts or current underwriting conclusions.
- **2002, Float / Terrorism and Insurance / Just Say No** (`index_split_037.html`): Growing float at low cost is difficult; a prior 13% cost of float and new contract exclusions expose real funding and tail-risk variability. Identify correlated/manmade aggregation and decline poorly understood risks. No authority here for assuming independence of crypto/stock/options exposures or capping catastrophic risk using a normal-volatility metric.
- **2002, Derivatives / Stock Options / EBIDTA** (`index_split_037.html`): Gen Re's derivatives exit and accounting criticism stress difficult unwinds and unreliable marks. Buffett expressly says employee options are not inherently bad and proposes value/cost-of-capital conditions: do not flatten distinct employee compensation and exchange-traded derivatives discussions into one universal claim. Depreciation, taxes and cash reinvestment are real economic costs; EBITDA is not free cash flow.
- **2002, Index Funds and PEs / Waiting for the Fat Pitch / Keys to Investing** (`index_split_037.html`): Buffett offers broad-index averaging as reasonable for a suitable long-run belief; Munger adds that excessive initial prices can still hurt. This limits a blanket anti-diversification reading of 1996. No single PE ratio suffices. Patience, temperament, competence, and stock-as-business ownership are supported; a defined technical entry trigger, liquidity threshold, Greeks rule, or expected win rate is not supplied.

## Trading-skill evaluation consequence

Use these readings as a clearly labeled historical rationale for patience, independent analysis, avoiding loss-recovery motives, understanding costs/leverage, checking incentives, and refusing poorly understood aggregated risks. Keep long-horizon ownership separate from intraday futures/options procedures. Any implementation thresholds, data-freshness gates, option-liquidity tests, stops, targets, Greeks constraints, backtests, and profitability estimates require independent evidence and explicit assumptions; this reading validates none of them. Current prices, fiscal ratios, broker rules, company moats and historical forecasts require fresh verification before present-day use. No live market or plugin operations were performed.
