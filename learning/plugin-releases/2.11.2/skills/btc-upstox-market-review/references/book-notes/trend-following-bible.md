# Andrew Abraham, *The Trend Following Bible*

## Source and review scope

Andrew Abraham, *The Trend Following Bible: How Professional Traders Compound Wealth and Manage Risk* (Wiley; copyright 2013; uploaded PDF filename labels 2012). The 210-page PDF was reviewed in reading order from the opening through the final index; PDF page 211 is blank. Review covered all extracted text, including the method chapters, hypothetical examples, self-reported trade journal, conclusion, disclosure and index. Chart examples were reviewed through their accompanying captions and prose; this is not an exhaustive full-resolution audit of every chart image. References below use the uploaded PDF's 1-based physical page numbers, not the book's printed folios.

This is a historical source review, not strategy validation, a replication, or evidence of a current BTC/India edge. The source itself labels its material educational and warns that hypothetical results have important limitations (PDF 205). Its methods, market data and personal settings are dated and mainly concern stocks and US futures.

## What the author proposes

The book presents trend following as a repeated, rules-based process that accepts frequent small losses and seeks to remain in occasional larger moves. It emphasizes defining the whole plan before entry, consistency, patience, position sizing, portfolio diversification and risk containment (PDF 24–63, 95–107, 147–170, 203–204). It also repeatedly describes prolonged drawdowns, whipsaws, gaps and years with few persistent trends; those are central costs of the approach, not footnotes (PDF 25–39, 64–94, 181–202).

The author's breakout example ranks a multi-market universe with smoothed rates of change, enters on channel breakouts with directional MACD context, limits concurrent positions, and protects trades first with a channel stop and then an ATR-based trailing stop after favorable movement (PDF 95–118). The retracement example uses a higher-timeframe trend filter, a lower-timeframe pullback/oscillator condition, a stop-entry beyond a recent two-bar swing, and an initial protective stop followed by ATR trailing management (PDF 120–146). The examples and parameters are the author's personal system choices, not universal rules.

The journal illustrates the distribution the author describes: numerous small losses, some small wins and occasional larger winners. It also records nine consecutive losing trades, later eighteen trades that did not work, a reported 2011 loss of about 11% for one account series, and gap losses larger than intended risk (PDF 171–202). These are author-reported historical examples, not audited or independently verified performance. The author's own disclosure says hypothetical results do not predict actual results (PDF 205).

## Useful review questions for a live signal

Use the book as a process lens only when trend continuation, breakout or pullback behavior is relevant:

- Is the proposed trend defined by observable swing structure or a disclosed indicator and timeframe, and is the signal based on completed bars?
- What exact event triggers entry, what invalidates the premise, and what protective exit is feasible after spread, slippage, gaps and funding?
- Is position size derived from a user-authorized loss budget and current contract specifications? A stop price does not cap losses through gaps or liquidation.
- Are exposures concentrated in correlated markets or the same underlying risk? Assess portfolio aggregation rather than counting positions as diversification.
- Can the plan survive an extended whipsaw/drawdown sequence, and is it being followed consistently rather than altered after recent losses?
- Has this specific adaptation been tested with point-in-time data, realistic costs and untouched out-of-sample periods?

These questions do not add confirmation by themselves. The source provides no current BTC price level, Delta contract data, Indian option quote, SMC/ICT detector, or validated BTC/India system. A trend-following framework can be inapplicable to a short-horizon mean-reversion or options-decay setup.

## Source problems and transfer limits

- Several worked entry/stop instructions appear directionally inconsistent. For example, the long retracement hard-stop summary says one tick above the prior two-bar low while the worked arithmetic uses a stop below it; short-side update wording also conflicts across the examples and summaries (PDF 140–146). The ATR stop comparisons and long/short wording are not consistently clear. Do not silently repair these into executable rules.
- The printed rate-of-change ranking expression appears to divide only the second of three terms by three, rather than the sum of all three (PDF 105). Treat it as an apparent source typo; verify any intended formula before coding.
- The risk-of-ruin expression and the accompanying simplified “number of bites” illustration do not establish a generally valid ruin probability. They omit key assumptions such as return distribution, dependence, changing equity, gaps, fees and liquidation (PDF 80–84). The author's 1% or 1.25% per-trade settings, sector caps, margin limits and contract limits are personal historical choices (PDF 84–95, 115–118, 196), not plugin defaults or recommendations.
- Statements that every trade is a 50/50 event or that the next trade is independent of the previous one overstate what can be inferred. Setup probabilities vary with conditional context, and trades can share regime, instrument and portfolio exposures (PDF 147–159, 187). Treat these as mindset rhetoric, not statistical proof.
- CTA performance histories, hypothetical curves and selected examples are vulnerable to survivorship, selection, hindsight and changing market structure; the book itself cautions against past-performance inference (PDF 64–94, 108–119, 205). Its historical manager returns and 2010–2011 journal cannot supply present-day odds.
- US futures sizing, contract multipliers, sessions, gap behavior and portfolio assumptions do not transfer directly to 24/7 BTC perpetuals, Indian derivatives, options premiums, funding, exchange liquidation rules or current fees. Adaptation requires separately specified rules and realistic, instrument-specific testing.

## Citation guidance

When relevant, cite the actual concept and physical PDF page, for example: “Abraham's breakout/trailing-stop framework (PDF 95–118; historical source rule, not validated for this instrument).” Pair it with current observed market evidence and a contrary interpretation. Do not cite the book as blanket confirmation, quote a backtest or return as a current probability, or imply that this review constitutes model training.
