## Source status and scope

PDF308–310 / printed291–293: author describes his swing approach as top-down context, pattern, defined stop, execution, active risk management, and one clean swing. This is a personal style, not a universal holding period. PDF309 / printed292 explicitly discloses some examples were not actually traded and were selected for teaching quality. The chapter is therefore illustrative and selection-biased: neither winners nor losses form a representative test sample. Setup/outcome chart pairs are deliberately used to expose hindsight distortion. Additional chart history cannot retroactively justify an entry at the truncated edge.

PDF309 / printed292: every entry has a predeclared stop. Author normally removes 25–50% at initial 1R, occasionally 20%; more aggressive management means removing less exposure, not raising initial account risk. Risk is consistent within trade classes. These are reported practices; the chapter supplies no independent evidence of an optimal partial-exit percentage or superior expectancy. PDF310 / printed293 warns that failed and unexpected patterns deserve study; indicators reproduced visually are often not discussed.

News-driven success is not proof the pattern caused the jump. Later downside/reentry lies outside author's chosen single-swing remit.

Relative-strength exemption and wider stop are discretionary, not a quantified classifier.

Visual inset confirms the distinct lower-time-frame washout; result is unusually clean and accelerates. Author calls it best case and qualifies apparent risk-free language for outliers. Gap/slippage risk survives a stop above entry.
- PDF317–320 / pp300–303 / figs10.7–10.8, AMZN high/tight: one-to-three-bar thrust and brief consolidation near extreme are author heuristics. Breakout gaps can worsen fills; prompt expansion expected. Wider risk stop again lowers nominal size, but discretionary exit may occur before it. Visual result reaches target yet then stalls, produces weak new highs, and gaps down. Textbook appearance did not produce ideal trend continuation. Later C is a weekly-scale opportunity, with a correspondingly larger stop; it cannot be substituted for the initial daily thesis.
- PDF320–322 / pp303–305 / figs10.9–10.10, coffee nested pullback: range spring plus breakout contextualize first tiny pause. - PDF322–324 / pp305–307 / figs10.11–10.12, EURUSD complex pullback: initial simple pullback can lose, but structure may develop into a two-leg correction and a fresh qualified entry. - PDF324–326 / pp307–309 / figs10.13–10.14, Treasury: outcome depends materially on breakout-entry versus close-entry location. - PDF326–328 / pp309–311 / figs10.15–10.16: continuation may stop at the prior swing; this can be a profitable managed trade but failed full-leg forecast. - PDF328–329 / pp311–312 / fig10.17, wheat: initial bearish move works before sharp opposing momentum. Author expects lowered stop to make the loss small; exact trailing method is explicitly absent. Visual shows sudden violent upthrust; cannot presume a perfect stop execution in real markets.

## Termination, failure tests, climaxes

- PDF330–333 / pp313–316 / figs10.19–10.20, ANN: prior sharp sell-off justifies watching a countertrend failure test in a leader; enter on reversal close versus intraday offers confirmation/location tradeoff. Stops near or just inside old extreme are author's execution preference. Visual result is expressly best case; later pullback deterioration motivates covering. Tight stop creates large position for fixed risk, while gaps can exceed risk; author suggests smaller class risk.
- PDF333–335 / pp316–318 / figs10.21–10.22, S&P: second-day failure test, target next day, later Flash Crash. Author explicitly denies prediction of the crash. Fragility assessment and a profitable position preceding an outlier are distinct from forecasting that outlier. Visual crash dwarfs original risk and should not be treated as typical payoff.
- PDF335 / p318: failure-test setup variants include unusually long late-trend consolidation, climax then retest, and trigger inside higher-time-frame structure. These are contextual variants, not extra precise entry algorithms. A late-trend long flag and breakout base can look similar; trend location is the differentiator.
- PDF336–338 / pp319–321 / figs10.23–10.24: cotton short stalls before 1R and resumes up; Fossil may create two full losses. Author's rough three-to-four-bar urgency for failure-test target is not universal timing calibration. Illiquidity and nonstandard setups complicate inference. Visual Fossil resumes uptrend, making repeated countertrend entries hazardous.
- PDF338–339 / pp321–322 / fig10.25, LBTYA: first short would be stopped by a slight higher high; separate valid second entry subsequently wins. Do not rewrite original stop to avoid first loss. Author describes second entry as nearly obligatory; this should transfer only as permission to reassess independently, never compulsory revenge/reentry without renewed validation and remaining risk capacity.
- PDF339 / p322: author advocates continuously working target orders in permitted overnight sessions; provider/venue/session constraints must govern adaptation. Thin-market touches are not guaranteed executable fills. Failure by adverse continuation gap can exceed predefined stop; smaller risk helps but does not cap loss.
- PDF340–342 / pp323–325 / fig10.26: stand-alone parabolic entries suffer unstable liquidity/spreads and timing risk. Wait for failure test retest or strong opposing thrust then Anti rather than trying to call the exact top. Silver report excerpts distinguish discomfort from an actual exit; tightened prior-day-low trail manages existing long through accelerating trend. Visual A–G and subsequent collapse support the sequence, but author calls top-adjacent exit fortuitous. Edited contemporary comments provide a process illustration, not an audited execution record.
- PDF342–343 / pp325–326 / figs10.27–10.28, cotton: classic climax and apparent failure test reaches 1R then fails as trend extends dramatically. Visual setup/result pair is crucial: terminal-looking move on truncated chart is early relative to eventual extension. Ultimate collapse offers no qualified entry under author's pattern rules. A large move need not be tradable.

## Anti and support/resistance examples

- PDF344–346 / pp327–329 / figs10.29–10.30, AG: climax then unusually sharp bearish impulse then reluctant bounce creates early potential new-trend pullback. Near stop above bounce or much farther stop above trend high are contextual alternatives; intermediate arbitrary stop asserted unhelpful. Visual multiple pauses do not invalidate immediately; lower failure test can motivate covering. This does not validate any particular near/far stop expectancy.
- PDF346–348 / pp329–331 / figs10.31–10.32, BIDU: strong band-hugging trend cannot be faded on extension alone; one anomalous down bar shifts context, then bearish pullback permits entry. New low in MACD corroborates, rather than independently authorizes. Later sharp reversal E ends single-swing trade even though downside later continues; F belongs to higher-time-frame structure.
- PDF348–350 / pp331–333 / figs10.33–10.34, EURUSD: unusually long/time-extended bounce from declining trend and new MACD high precede nested complex buy Anti. Late entry after large D changes economics; author invokes asset-class tendencies to accept it. Those historical FX-vs-equity tendencies must be retested for BTC/Indian instruments, not imported. PDF349 says stop 'somewhere above 1.20'; this is a broad illustrative level, not enough for an executable tick-specific rule.
- PDF350–353 / pp333–336 / figs10.35–10.37: YGE buy Anti stagnates (weak setup thrust); coffee sell Anti finds prior-swing support then sharply reclaims it; USDCHF buy Anti loses fully when downward momentum resumes. Visuals confirm three different paths and why profitable first-target handling is not guaranteed. Author explicitly notes imperfect-looking patterns need not have lower expectancy: appearance grades are not empirical probability estimates.
- PDF354–356 / pp337–339 / figs10.38–10.39, VHC: early spring entry inside a base has better location but breakout may still fail; initial D breakout fails, tighter later base finally breaks F. Visual spring E tiny penetration flagged by author as potentially unrealistic entry. Risk belongs to selected higher-time-frame thesis; using small-time-frame trigger with daily-range stop lowers size. Adding then breakout failure may warrant exiting more than added, an author's discretionary management rule.
- PDF356–358 / pp339–341 / figs10.40–10.41, JPM: weakening bounces and clean loss of support precede difficult early short B; later simple C pullback is clearer. Explicit real-time versus hindsight distinction. Visual B trades back above broken level; breakout level is not a magical boundary, and postbreakout price structure matters more than exact level relationship. Early anticipatory upside-base trade could have been reasonable and losing.
- PDF358–360 / pp341–343 / fig10.42, DFS: breakouts can fail at level, in first consolidation, or before leaving base at all. Visual DFS includes both downside springs and failed upside breaks. Distinguish ordinary retest from decisive failure without assuming all recrosses invalidate. Initial clean breakout pullbacks are asserted rarely complex; stop should reference actual pattern geometry. That frequency claim lacks sample statistics here.

## What is missing and must not be invented

No reproducible numeric detector for change of character, mature trend, quality, strong/reluctant thrust, climax, spring depth, acceptable compression, valid extension, or expiry. No tested thresholds linking MACD/Keltner patterns to probability. No exhaustive trades, costs, spread/slippage distribution, funding, borrow, taxes, liquidity filter, portfolio correlation, execution audit, or out-of-sample expectancy. Three-to-four bars and one-to-three-bar thrusts are pattern-specific heuristics. Partial exits and tightly trailed stops require explicitly tested assumptions; the chapter's strong language does not prove optimality. Price bars alone do not observe trapped traders' intentions, weak hands, accumulation, or equilibrium; these are explanatory hypotheses consistent with some paths.

## Transferable process for BTC / Indian-market review (future design only)

1. State observed higher/trading/trigger time frames separately, then state conditional thesis and alternative failure path. Label author-derived interpretation versus measured observations.
2. Record exact entry method, initial invalidation/stop, unit economics, costs, target/partial policy, expiry and follow-through expectation before outcome. A delayed close fill is a different trade from breakout touch.
3. Treat continuation, termination/failure-test, Anti and breakout contexts as related templates with explicit failure families: stall, prior-swing rejection, strong opposing momentum, adverse gap, and failure in base. No forced setup when evidence is ambiguous.
4. Separate existing-position management from permission for a fresh entry; a useful holding pattern need not support adding risk. Reentry requires a newly valid setup, respected prior stop, and remaining risk budget.
5. Account for venue-specific sessions, overnight gaps, continuous BTC funding/liquidation and outages, lot size and option payoff/Greeks/liquidity where relevant. Underlying-chart 1R is not option premium 1R. The source provides no option-specific rules.
6. Use withheld-right-edge replay plus failures and nontrades in later validation. Do not infer win rate from these curated examples or claim the Flash Crash / final climax was predictable. Transfer process discipline; separately test the instrument/time-frame implementation.
