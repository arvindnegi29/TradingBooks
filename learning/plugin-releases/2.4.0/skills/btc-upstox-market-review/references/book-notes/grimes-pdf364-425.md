# Independent source review: Grimes PDF 364–425

## Coverage
Read every supplied text page sequentially, 364–425 inclusive, in six untruncated bounded reads. PDF391 is blank. This covers Chapter11 (printed347–373), Chapter12 (375–398), AppendixA (399–408). ## Transferable source-grounded process
- Ch11 PDF365,384–385: Psychology, confidence, intuition and diligence cannot create an edge. First establish a method's market basis; then evaluate consistent execution. Use an operational process, not psychological diagnosis.
- Ch11 PDF365–374: A profitable outcome can follow a bad decision and a loss can follow a good decision. Preserve contemporaneous observations and evaluate distributions rather than rewriting a system after one event. Author's 20–30-trade learning and 50-trade change illustrations (PDF374) are heuristics, not statistically sufficient universal sample sizes.
- Ch11 PDF370–373: Keep a full record to counter selective recall, recent-event overweighting, hindsight and spectacular-outlier fixation. Streaks can be ordinary randomness or regime-dependent; do not infer that a win is due after losses.
- Ch11 PDF371: Stop distance and account risk differ. With sizing held to a risk budget, a narrow stop is not automatically safer; frequent stop-outs may undermine expectancy. Do not tighten stops merely to make a trade fit.
- Ch11 PDF377–380: Treat intuitive observations as fallible hypotheses requiring rational review, not as proof of a forecasting edge. New or unverified intuition is no basis for removing controls.
- Ch11 PDF389–390: For hybrid discretionary/systematic work, compare interventions to the unchanged baseline. Separate method performance from implementation variance. Consistent schedules, records, reviews and rule use reduce confounding.
- Ch12 PDF398–400: Maintain an overarching business plan and a more operational trading plan. Define research/review schedule, eligible patterns, activity limits, concurrent positions, aggregate capital at risk, margin use, loss-stop policy, entry/exit/partial management and decision cadence. Version material changes rather than silently rewriting history.
- Ch12 PDF400–401: Review at a cadence suited to strategy frequency; author's monthly/quarterly examples are contextual. Review equity curve, setup categories and individual trades, document recurring errors and choose a few specific next-period improvements. Separate routine monitoring from substantive rule revision.
- Ch12 PDF402–405: Keep narrative observations separate from trade-specific accounting. Record market facts and personal reactions distinctly. A daily plan should cover market context, existing positions, possible new entries, levels, related markets and multiple time frames. Preserve plans to detect drift.
- Ch12 PDF405: Reconcile independent records against broker accounting, particularly assignments, rolls, conversions and corporate actions. Freeze setup classification at decision time: hindsight relabeling corrupts attribution. Three–five setup groups are an illustrative compromise, not a mandated taxonomy.

## Statistics and risk attribution
Ch12 PDF406–411 gives a deliberately theoretical 68-trade, four-system example, not evidence of a tradable current strategy. Table12.1: net total3158, mean46, SD600, 58.8% wins, one-tailed t-test p=.263. SystemC has 81.3% wins yet mean69 with average win361 and loss1194; hit rate alone conceals payoff asymmetry. Table12.2 divides each trade's dollar outcome by its initial dollar risk (the book calls this %R, but it is a dimensionless R multiple, not a percentage unless multiplied by100). Risk-normalized average is approximately0.3R, SD0.8R; variable sizing had obscured the example's risk-normalized result. Preserve BOTH dollar/net-equity results and R results; normalization does not repair harmful sizing or establish achievable future returns.

Figure12.1 is cumulative CLOSED-trade P&L, explicitly not daily marked-to-market equity (PDF407). Figures12.2–12.3 show large dollar losses becoming ordinary R losses. They motivate questions about sizing and behavior but cannot establish causal or psychological explanations. Figure12.4's hypothetical 1%-fixed-fractional curve ends22935 versus actual3158 (PDF410–411); it is a counterfactual accounting illustration, not a recommendation to risk1%, and the hypothetical compounds sequentially rather than summing R multiples. Concurrent exposure, costs, execution, path and contract constraints need independent treatment.

PDF411–414 describes diagnostic control charts: plot individual period P&L, trailing mean and SD bands; calculate reference mean/SD using prior observations (lag one period) to avoid today's extreme diluting its own flag. Author's10–50-observation lookbacks and1.5–3SD multipliers are examples; not universal parameters or probability envelopes. Returns need not be normal and trading does not meet tightly controlled manufacturing assumptions. A rolling high/low and win-ratio chart can add context. These charts generate investigation questions; they do not diagnose causes or authorize risk increases.

## AppendixA mechanics and limitations
- PDF417–418: Last trade may be stale and unexecutable. Distinguish live bid/ask/last, quote timing, available size and book depth. A tight spread alone is not enough to demonstrate executable liquidity; a shallow book can move substantially under a large order.
- PDF419: Market orders favor immediate execution over price control; passive limits may never fill. The text oversimplifies by defining limits as passive: marketable limits also exist. Never copy this simplification into execution instructions.
- PDF420: Observed buying/selling can represent hedges or spread legs, so it does not prove an outright directional belief. Relative-value calculations require actual weights/multipliers/currency treatment; prose switches between price-difference spreads and equal-weight percentage outcomes.
- PDF421–425: Tick bars aggregate transaction counts rather than uniform elapsed time; time bars aggregate duration. Candles and OHLC bars encode the same four prices, visually confirmed FigureA.3. Volume/range bars and profile views are additional aggregation choices, not independent confirming evidence. Do not apply the book's blanket claim that range bars make backtesting virtually impossible without checking actual construction and historical/live consistency. MarketProfile descriptions blur time-at-price and volume-at-price; label the actual input and algorithm instead of equating them.

## Source issues and nontransferable claims
Ch11–12 contain broad evolutionary, neuroscience, chemical-state and personality claims, anecdotal recovery steps, and speculative estimates of failure/success rates and learning timelines. These are not validated clinical findings in this review. Avoid importing them as diagnoses, rules for any user's mental health, or guarantees that persistence eventually works. Do not reproduce risky cold-exposure advice or instructions to execute a small real-money trade as a psychological test (PDF369). An operational pause on new risk with user-chosen re-evaluation is sufficient; existing-position actions must respect an independently established plan.

Author's 2011-era market rankings, account-size/dollar-loss illustrations, fee estimates, historical fund comparisons and prediction that human scalping might disappear are context-bound, not current facts (PDF386–388,394–398,400). Risk learning budgets are not universally suitable, and continued losses do not justify continued funding. PDF371's expected-dollar-value gambling comparison ignores utility/capital constraints; translate to risk-aware expectancy rather than declaring every trader obliged to choose the higher-dollar-EV gamble.

P-values in PDF407/409 are conditional tail probabilities under a null/model, not the probability that the trader lacks an edge. Rounded p=.000 means below display precision, not zero. Small class sizes (especially D:N6), dependence, nonstationarity, selection, multiple comparisons, heavy tails and varying opportunity sets limit t-tests. Inferential checks should state assumptions and uncertainty; an in-sample attractive subgroup requires independent validation. The book's categorical statement that normalized analysis reveals a clear edge is too strong to transfer unchanged.

## BTC / Indian-market adaptation cautions (implementation judgment, not claims from this book)
Apply mechanics to the exact instrument and venue: cash equity, futures, perpetual or option are not interchangeable. Verify current lot/multiplier/tick/expiry/settlement rules and fee/tax/funding schedules from fresh sources before numbers. BTC's continuous trading needs an explicit time zone and day boundary; Indian exchange sessions, overnight gaps, expiry and exchange holidays require explicit session treatment. Option outcome distributions and initial-risk denominators need special care: underlying stop distance is not an option's guaranteed loss; nonlinear valuation, spread, liquidity, margin and assignment/settlement can dominate. A stop is a conditional order/plan, not a guaranteed realized loss cap.

For read-only review workflows, preserve manual execution. Do not imply book-based chart labels establish current trade eligibility, freshness, executable option pricing or a proved local edge. Use current provider timestamps; require fresh bid/ask, spread/depth and contract details for conditional plans. Record rejected/no-trade cases and contemporaneous labels alongside any actual authorized trade records; do not fabricate filled trades or performance. Backtests must be user-requested and reproducible, with costs and time-aligned input data, rather than launched merely because this chapter discusses statistics.
