## Chapter 8: trade management (PDF248–278)

- PDF248–250/printed231–233: a management rule must fit the entry signal's predictive horizon and typical magnitude. A short-lived signal cannot automatically support distant targets. Stop distance, target, hit rate and position size interact; personal comfort and discipline do not establish profitability. Fixed price-percentage stops ignore volatility differences. Volatility-based stops adapt distance to typical movement, but constants are choices requiring validation.
- PDF250–251/233–234 figure8.1: initial stop can refer to a nearby meaningful pivot or a more distant prior pivot, with smaller size for wider distance. Ragged support calls for attention to the extreme low and fake breaks. Author's 2–4+ ATR illustration is personal heuristic, not universal standard. Chapter9 later warns against stops inside one average bar range: these are related noise cautions, not an internally consistent mandatory numeric threshold.
- PDF252–253/235–236: risk-multiple partial targets, structural targets and open-ended exits are alternatives; partial exits reduce later exposure and therefore reduce the payoff of large winners. Author's quarter-to-third at1R and reduced balance at2R are examples only. Prefer conservative limit-fill assumptions in research; a price touch does not establish a fill. Structural targets may warrant placing an order inside obvious resistance/support. Historical venue queue assumptions and all-session order suggestions cannot be generalized without checking current instrument rules.
- PDF254–256/237–239 figures8.2: moving average/trendline stops can be arbitrary and susceptible to false breaks. SAR tightens through an accelerating mechanism; chandelier references ATR distance from an extreme without SAR acceleration. SAR need not imply reversing when used only for an exit. Learn actual calculations and test artificial cases; these pages do not supply an implementation-ready recurrence. Do not cherry-pick strongly trending samples when evaluating trailing stops. The author's discretionary-outperformance opinion is not demonstrated evidence.
- PDF257–263/240–246 figures8.3–8.4: define total cash risk, ultimate stop, maximum size and average entry for planned scale-ins. Taking losses on part of a position changes later P&L sensitivity and can leave losses even if price recovers; taking profits sacrifices participation. Separate a planned multi-piece initial entry from adding new risk after initial size is reached. Re-entry and pyramiding require explicit combined-position accounting or independent entry rules. Reverse pyramids become top-heavy; progressively smaller additions reduce that feature but do not remove loss risk. Calling residual exposure 'free' or 'risk-free' is unsafe: open profits can disappear and execution can exceed stops. Time stops are hypotheses tied to observed signal decay, not an invariant best-trades-work-immediately rule.
- PDF264–267/247–250 figure8.5: tightening reduces adverse-distance exposure but can reduce hit probability. Tight stops can act as deliberate profit taking in climactic movement; consolidation noise can defeat them. New stops should follow predeclared structure rules rather than emotional aversion to giving back gains. Author's two exceptions for re-widening remain within initial risk; a smaller incomplete position with a farther stop can preserve cash budget. This is not permission to increase risk. Gap-through-stop loss can exceed intended risk; the historical gap-reversal discussion is not a current India/BTC rule.
- PDF268–270/251–253: simultaneously held trades constitute a portfolio. Risk concentration can survive apparently different tickers, longs and shorts; diversification and hedges may fail under stress. Author's group limits2–3X equities,1.5–2X other groups and illustrative portfolio5X are personal examples, not user defaults. Require user-specific total/group budgets. 'All correlations tend toward1' is an overgeneralization; operationally stress common factors and adverse co-movement rather than assume a universal coefficient.
- PDF271–275/254–258 figure8.6: maintain dated risk/P&L record, strategy tag, exact symbol, side, net size, entry accounting basis, reference price, realized/unrealized/total P&L, initial-R P&L, stop(s), current-price-to-stop intended loss and total trade P&L at stop. Distinguish original risk from current open exposure and distinguish both from maximum possible loss. Multiple stop levels need separate quantities. Normalized volatility helps compare unlike prices; a standard-deviation move is descriptive, not a tail bound. Range-location formula=(last−low)/(high−low), with zero-range case needing explicit handling if implemented. Intraday/day definitions must be fixed.
- PDF275–278/258–261: adding versus taking liquidity creates price/urgency tradeoffs and adverse selection. Marketable limits cap price but can leave unfilled residual; markets can fill far from expected price. Stops can prioritize exit over price certainty. Thin-market displayed liquidity may disappear; historical hidden-order tactics are not instructions for these venues. Correct operational mistakes promptly under an established incident procedure. Author's recommendation to skip basic breaks is not a necessary trading principle and should not enter a skill.

## Chapter 9: risk and position sizing (PDF280–307)

- PDF280–284/263–267: define invalidation before entry; choose size to fit risk budget, rather than alter valid stop to force a desired size. Basic linear-unit size = cash budget / cash loss per unit at intended stop. R is the initial allocated cash risk, fixed as denominator for comparable realized results. Recompute actual position exposure after every size/stop change; do not silently redefine original R.
- PDF281–283/264–266: binary Kelly f=(bp−q)/b, b=average win/average loss, p+q=1 in the modeled binary case. It assumes sufficiently known, relevant payoff probabilities and dependence structure; estimated historical edge, changing regimes, serial dependence, jumps and unbounded losses can invalidate aggressive sizing. No Kelly or optimal-f output should become a live recommendation from the book alone. Author's under1% conservative/3% aggressive labels describe his judgment; not defaults or safety guarantees.
- PDF283–284/266–267: drawdown recovery r=d/(1−d), with d expressed as fraction. Sequential fixed-fraction losses yield remaining wealth W0(1−f)^t; loss fraction=1−(1−f)^t. Concurrent positions all sized on the same starting equity add their cash budgets rather than sequentially shrink after each loss. This distinction is crucial for correlated losses.
- PDF285–294/268–277: the Monte Carlo model is a synthetic example only: initial100k,250 trades,1000 paths,independent equal-probability wins+1.2R/losses−1R, before real-world frictions. Mean expectancy0.1R/trade. Fixed cash2k gives nominal expected profit50k before bankruptcy absorption, and empirical terminal mean149259. Tables compare cash risks, fractional risks and random sizes; full path behavior is different from terminal outcome. Table column 'terminal DD≥75%' is starting-equity terminal loss, not peak-to-trough maximum drawdown. Bankruptcy absorption applies to fixed-cash paths; zeros in high fractional table may be display rounding, not literal mathematical zero.
- PDF291–294/274–277: fractional sizing gives right-skewed terminal wealth; standard deviation and coefficient of variation alone cannot express downside risk. Mean can rise while median collapses with excessive fraction; rare extreme winners cannot be marketed as typical outcomes. Fractions approaching zero wealth still constitute practical ruin. Do not infer book sample frequency as a calibrated future probability. One thousand paths is illustrative and sampling error remains. Reproducible seeds/sequences matter, but no seed/raw paths supplied here permit exact reproduction.
- PDF294–295/277–278: random risk changes add variability; discretionary size overlays need objective incremental evidence relative to a baseline. Capital allocation%,margin% and equal-volatility sizing are distinct from stop-loss risk. Quiet compressed markets can produce deceptively large equal-volatility positions before a jump. These methods do not automatically measure true risk.
- PDF296–300/279–283: both loss probability and loss magnitude matter. Expected loss=p(loss)×E[loss|loss], while low-frequency high-impact losses remain consequential. Expected value=pwin×meanwin−ploss×meanloss (before costs). Hit rate alone and nominal reward/risk alone cannot establish positive expectancy. Historical standard deviation can hide asymmetric option-selling tail risk. Author's normal-loss-as-business-expense framing is psychological; planned losses remain capital risk and no finite sample or positive estimated expectancy guarantees recovery/profit.
- PDF300–307/283–290: possible lack of edge, execution slippage/missed orders/size errors, infrastructure outage, disaster, regime change, tails, common factors, disappearing liquidity, margin/regulatory changes and trade busts all matter. Keep accurate results and monitor degradation under a predeclared process, without assuming every loss proves edge decay. Model and realized performance diverge. Historical assertions about regulatory behavior, hedging expense or market structure are contextual 2012 commentary, not current findings.

## Independent arithmetic and source defects

1. PDF272/255 accounting example is mislabeled. Buy1@10,buy1@15,sell1@20: true FIFO realizes10 and leaves cost15. Book's stated 'FIFO' realizes5/leaves10, which is LIFO. Average cost realizes7.50/leaves12.50. At any common mark M, total P&L=M−5 for all three. Visual original confirms text error, not extraction artifact.
2. PDF283/266 printed formula labels (1−N)^t 'compound loss'; it is retained equity factor. Five4% losses leave81.53726976%, losing18.46273024%, consistent with rounded18.5% prose only after taking complement.
3. Recovery arithmetic checks:5%/(95%)=5.2631579%,20%/80%=25%,50%/50%=100%. Source's percent notation must be interpreted as fraction inputs.
4. PDF284/267 example checks:100k×1%=1000; entry50−stop47.5=2.5; size400 linear shares, before commissions/slippage. It does not directly translate to contracts/options without unit multipliers and pricing.
5. PDF285–286 expectancy checks:0.5×1.2−0.5×1=0.1R;250×2k×0.1=50k. For nonabsorbed model exact SD=2200√250≈34785; table's33760 is a sample statistic, plausibly sampling variation and not independent reproduction. Fixed2% theoretical expected wealth before friction=100k×1.002^250≈164789.82, versus sample163033. This is not a BTC forecast.
6. PDF290/273 arithmetic typo:25k/2k=12.5, not printed11.5. PDF289/272 says cash5k gives 'almost50% more profit' than2k; empirical profits121894 vs49259 imply about147.45% more profit; terminal wealth rises48.66%. It conflates profit and wealth.
7. PDF292/275:100k×0.95^250≈0.26971265, rounded to cents0.27, whereas source says about0.28. Broad practical-ruin lesson unchanged.
8. PDF293/276 Kelly example checks:((1.2×0.5)−0.5)/1.2=0.0833333. That is a toy-model growth optimum, not a recommended fraction.
9. PDF287/270 claims a balance12400 means87.5% drawdown; loss versus initial100k is87.6%. A true peak-to-trough drawdown requires path peak information. Treat approximate narration only.
10. PDF292/275 distribution wording is approximate: with fixed count250 and binary outcomes, terminal wealth is discrete; log wealth is affine in a binomial win count, approximately normal, not exactly normal; wealth is correspondingly approximately lognormal. Fixed cash terminal wealth is also discrete and bankruptcy absorption alters its distribution. Do not promote qualitative curve overlays or sample normality tests into exact distribution assumptions.
11. PDF261/244 half off at+1R then refill half at entry yields realized+0.5R, current full-position stop risk−1R, combined−0.5R (ignoring costs). This checks the arithmetic but not claimed strategy efficacy. PDF276/259 spread example also checks:0.10−0.02−0.02=0.06 win,−0.10−0.02−0.02=−0.14 loss;50/50 expectancy becomes−0.04 before other costs.
12. Figure8.6 PDF271 row labels and sign convention are readable, but rounded underlying marks/average costs can prevent exact reconciliation from displayed cells. The intended-current-risk columns are often negative-sign loss entries. An implementation should use explicitly defined signed/unsigned fields, not blindly subtract a negative loss from P&L. Prose definition 'PLStop=currenttotal−Risk' assumes a positive risk magnitude despite figure's loss-sign notation. Do not copy ambiguous convention.

## BTC/India applicability boundaries for a future review skill

These are transfer candidates requiring current provider/venue validation, not live trade advice or claims of built capability. Review can separate setup/invalidation from any user-authorized cash budget, show conditional size only with known equity/budget and instrument details, and otherwise avoid guessing. Perpetuals require contract value, settlement currency, leverage/liquidation and funding context; margin is collateral rather than stop-loss budget. Indian futures/options require exact current symbol, expiry, lot/unit multipliers, tick size and margin rules. Options have nonlinear/time/IV exposure: an underlying invalidation is not a guaranteed premium exit or max loss; premium,spread width,settlement and multi-leg fill risks need instrument-specific treatment.

Preserve manual-only/read-only boundaries. A market-review connector lacking account/position data cannot report actual portfolio concentration, current equity, actual margin or realized risk; it can ask for user-supplied values or label illustrative scenarios. Flag shared BTC directional exposure across perps/options and shared India index/sector exposure where supported; do not claim measured correlation from tickers. Stress simultaneous stop-outs and gap/jump/slippage beyond stops, plus liquidity at intended size. Current bid/ask/depth/spread data and freshness are prerequisites for execution-sensitive commentary. Define BTC session/timezone and India sessions before ATR/day metrics; do not directly transplant US overnight order/gap lore. Use long enough and appropriately out-of-sample evidence before claims that a management overlay, target, time stop, indicator, or pattern has an edge. No current law, cost, venue feature or provider response was verified in this bounded source-reading task.
