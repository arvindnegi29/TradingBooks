## Core source constraints
Chapter6 introduction (PDF166/p149) explicitly treats templates as expressions of possible pressure imbalance, not profitable visual patterns by themselves. Shapes define entries/risk but need wider structure and psychology. Chapter6 summary (PDF204–205/p187–188) reduces them to trend continuation, S/R holding, S/R breaking. Author excludes undifferentiated range trading, simply buying a support line, and fading merely because price looks extended. This materially constrains skill additions: name a candidate with context and invalidation, not a detected edge.

Chapter7 (PDF206–207/p189–190) favors a few understood tools over a pile of opinions or indicators. Confirmation is not an automatic requirement to multiply indicators. Statements of reliability/probability throughout are author claims, not evidence of current BTC/India performance.

## Chapter6 templates and conditional use

1. **Failure test** (PDF167–170/p150–153). Context: mature/extended trend or a heavily watched consolidation breakout that fails; momentum divergence often accompanies best examples, but is not mechanically compulsory. Short trigger: trade beyond clearly defined resistance then close back underneath on the same or following bar. Reverse for long. Enter on reclaim/failure bar CLOSE. Hard stop beyond the entire excursion extreme, which can belong to previous bar, not necessarily signal bar. Do not average losers. Author suggests smaller setup risk due to overnight gaps, partial profit around1R, and discretionary remainder rather than assuming major reversal. It should move away promptly; one-to-three trading bars to profitability is source guidance; consolidation at failed level is adverse. A second failure after stop-out can justify reentry; budget combined attempts instead of independently risking full allocation twice.

2. **Pullback via support/resistance** (PDF171–179/p154–162). Needs real trend/impulse likely to continue, preceding leg at least as strong as comparable legs, no obvious contradictory divergence/overextension. Pullback should reduce activity/ranges and lack forceful countertrend momentum; lower volume is common, not indispensable (PDF172 contains an apparent omitted 'not' in its concluding volume phrase, reconciled by surrounding discussion). Sloping S/R makes simple fixed-line entries/stops hazardous. Preferred skilled trigger: washout through pullback channel then immediate recovery within a few bars (PDF173). Direct bottom-of-pullback entry is possible but vulnerable to being washed out. Three-or-more support tests with lower highs into it are adverse (PDF174), not impossibility. Stops must reflect invalidation/noise; author favors farther stops with small unspecified jitter, never a published universal tick/ATR formula. Stop distance is not account risk; size to risk. Targets: preceding setup-leg extreme, approximate measured move zone, partials at1R/2R with structure-aware adjustment (PDF176–178). For bullish AB impulse and C retracement, D=C+(B−A); do not use future C before recognizable. Treat D as zone, no mystical exactness.

3. **Pullback via lower-time-frame breakout** (PDF179–182/p162–165). Same context. Trade breakout of real internal structure, not arbitrary previous-bar high. Example daily/78-minute; other examples1/5,10/30,daily/weekly,weekly/monthly illustrate related scales, not universal parameter requirements. Two–three bars sharing a high may define valid local resistance, but is occasional. Momentum confirmation costs entry location and usually requires larger structural stop. Author argues against repeated tiny-stop attempts due to noise, spread, multiple costs and missing eventual move. Gap-through-entry handling must be planned; no one execution policy supplied. Same pullback targets.

4. **Complex pullback** (PDF182–186/p165–169). Two countertrend legs, separated by temporary primary-trend resumption: an ABCD lower-time-frame structure. Can be hidden as one/two with-trend candles amid countertrend candles. These are clues, not a sufficient detector. Usual pullback tests apply, but extended complex consolidation can rehabilitate a post-climax continuation scenario where simple first pullback is unsuitable. Entry via support or breakout; approximate equality of corrective legs suggests a potential region, not certainty. Stop beyond second-leg/consolidation extreme is more defensible than in simple pullback. Three-plus-leg corrections are possible but not focus. Define cumulative risk when failed simple-pullback attempt turns into complex setup. Partial policy may retain more for potential extension, but added complexity needs justification.

5. **Anti** (PDF187–190/p170–173). Ordered sequence: reason to suspect old trend termination (divergence, higher-time-frame extension, double test, failure test); distinctly stronger countertrend impulse/change of character; controlled FIRST retracement of that impulse. Entry via prior pullback methods; anywhere-in-pullback with stop beyond trend extreme is a less precise alternative. Cannot label every MACD fast/slow disagreement an Anti. Stop beyond old-trend extreme or closer pullback invalidation, strictly respected. Author suggests33–66% partial at first target. MMO exhaustion is crucial: two opposing legs that stall there may instead be complex correction in original trend. Reassess remaining risk; continuation beyond target may be major reversal but is not assumed. Raschke stochastic origin acknowledged; author prefers price structure to indicator-only screens. PDF189 description says down leg stronger than previous 'upswings'; context implies compare character of opposing movement, so avoid literal automated direction comparison from that sentence.

6. **Prebreakout base** (PDF191–197/p174–180). Important visible level plus compressed base near range edge, or rising lows into resistance (reverse downside). PDF193 prose says successively higher 'highs' but explanation and Fig6.18/PDF194 explicitly say higher LOWS—use visual/semantic correction, not high sequence. Prior sustained penetration can weaken level; tiny failed probes need not. Base entries: bid low with missed-winner/adverse-selection tradeoff; close after spring; discretionary entry within range. No precise width/duration thresholds supplied. Separate prebreakout invalidation from postbreakout handling. Rapid probe below base may recover; sustained consolidation below support contradicts long. Do not automatically raise stop to breakout line: good breakouts can retest through it. Partial around1R; rest managed as emerging trend. Range-width MMO may understate expansion after compressed volatility.20/55-day Turtle channels are a different trend-capture system, not a validated substitute for these visible levels.

7. **First pullback after breakout** (PDF198–199/p181–182). Requires break of meaningful level with convincing activity beyond it and then controlled correction. Use normal pullback trigger/stops/targets; actual breakout line is usually poor universal stop. Prebreakout base pivot can be last-ditch invalidation/reference for sizing; loss below it argues failed breakout. First attempts preferred over complicated serial retries. Retain some potential trend participation. Higher-time-frame support can help but is not mandatory by fiat.

8. **Failed breakout** (PDF200–203/p183–186). Diagnose breakout quality and first reaction, not mere reentry inside broken level. Strong adverse first reaction/failed pullback can signal failure; failure test can provide trigger. Confirmation versus location remains subjective; author calls this most complex/least defined setup. Ultimate stop beyond initial pullback-thrust extreme, directional symmetry required (text writes 'above' in generic discussion). Strict stop/no averaging. Partial near1R, often more than half, geometry adjusted; possible later range absorption or much larger reversal. Source phrase 'most breakouts fail' is not a measured modern instrument-specific probability.

## Chapter7 confirmation tools through PDF247

**Moving averages** (PDF207–212/p190–195): no magic length, unsupported automatic support/resistance or crossing edge. Side-of-average residence and slope summarize trends; MA length changes represented timescale. Chopping near intermediate MA is avoidance context, while mere departure does not prove imbalance. MA may discourage overextended pullback entries but 'far' needs definition. Mechanical slope filters lag and may exclude beginning/permit late losers.100+period and<10period references are illustrative timescales, not recommended system parameters.

**Channels** (PDF212–218/p195–201): bands are alerts, not price barriers. Discussed Bollinger default20SMA±2SD and author empirical88% close inclusion vs claimed normal96%; not transferable guarantee. Prefers modified Keltner true-range response. Table7.1 (PDF215/p198) is explicitly2.25ATR channels, based on2,403,774 bars across large/small US stocks,futures,forex,random data. Mean range-inside percentages87.7/85.4/85.9/89.8/86.8; mean free-bar frequencies3.4/4.3/3.8/2.3/3.8%. Inside is share of TOTAL BAR RANGE, not percentage of closing prices/bars. Free bar: whole range outside channel. Random data similarity proves these statistics are not profitability evidence. Precise basis/ATR lengths/smoothers need AppendixB; do not invent from this chapter. Author's85–90% containment is historical broad-sample descriptive calibration, not universal confidence interval.

Band fade versus subsequent mid-band trend entry captures impulse/correction; Fig7.8 example requires CLOSE outside band rather than touch (PDF216). These illustrations do not override Chapter6 need for structured setup/risk. Persistent band slide indicates extreme pressure, difficult entries and snapback risk; default stand aside absent existing-position management (PDF217–218). Fast spikes through opposite bands imply contraction/triangle-like chop, avoidance not automatic reversal entry.

**Modified MACD** (PDF219–230/p202–213): momentum proxy derived from price; cannot literally predict before price. Do not transplant its rules to RSI/stochastic. Fast-line new extreme relative to recent history can support NEXT retracement, but climax invalidates naive continuation.40-bar comparison and percentile/top/bottom decile are optional starting suggestions, not proven detector. Full parameters/construction deferred AppendixB. Only slow line explicitly16-period SMA of fast line in this range (PDF229).

Divergence (PDF223–228): compare separate real price swings; no intervening retracement=no valid divergence. Trends can defeat many divergences; no automatic reversal or exit. Approximate reset when price returns to20-period/intermediate MA, fast line crosses zero, or10+bars pass (PDF226); heuristic tied to this indicator, not timeless expiration. Price extremes versus close-derived indicator is a measurement inconsistency; midpoint/typical-price or high/low-specific variants are suggestions requiring complexity/value testing. Fast line held extended can be genuine strength, not fade. A near-zero reset may help ensure distinct swings.

Fast hook (PDF228–229/p211–212): example short is first CLOSED bar causing line downturn AFTER price has penetrated lower band and absent divergence. Chart arrows look favorable partly because entries occur at close near low, not at arrow/high. Author explicitly cautions handpicked chart, ambiguous final divergence, and incomplete system. Slow slope/position are lagged context; long bias example slow above zero with fast below and hook is candidate context, not full mechanical strategy. Fully specify penetration recency, swing criteria, hook equality, entry execution and exits before any test.

**Multiple time frames** (PDF230–244/p213–227): define trading/higher/lower with neighboring factors usually3–5; not universal fixed list. Higher timeframe does not always dominate: identify current controlling structure, permit 'unclear'. HTF can negate trade without establishing opposite trade. Exhaustion can contradict trend pullbacks; HTF valid pullback/drive can support otherwise mediocre LTF setup; contextual target completion removes bias. HTF ranges can contain clean LTF trends, so avoid a global 'HTF range=no trade' filter. Lower-timeframe break at structural tipping point can time entry; lower detail also adds noise and overreaction risk. Do not force a confluence that is not obvious. PDF237 lists not-third-or-later-leg as high-probability characteristic; earlier chapter permits mature complex corrections, so treat age as caution/context rather than hard universal exclusion.

**Relative strength beginning** (PDF244–247/p227–230): cross-market relative performance, not RSI. In downtrend 'leader' may mean strongest downside participant. Seek stronger candidates on weakness and weaker on strength with actual pattern trigger; do not blindly chase ranked leader or buy laggard because cheap. Anchored ROC at common meaningful benchmark pivot; arbitrary start effects and endpoint blindness matter. Multi-horizon average/weighting is an exploratory alternative, no prescribed horizons or weights. Rank, rebalance, execution trigger and exit form joint design. Leadership rotates among group; one rank drop is not automatic invalidation. No proof of institutional identity from price alone; pressure attribution remains interpretive.

## Causal timing, implementation gaps, validation needed
- Use only completed bars for close-defined failures/hooks; at decision timestamp a higher-time-frame bar may be incomplete. Store confirmed-vs-forming status. A historical final weekly tail/MACD value cannot be supplied to earlier daily/intraday decision.
- Pivot-based S/R, AB/C targets and divergence require explicit causal swing confirmation. Backfilled zigzag/extrema using future bars invalidate historical entry times. Record level origin, known-at time, timestamp/timezone and update rule.
- Define trend, 'significant', immediate/few bars, mature/climax, compression, controlled correction, sloping line fit, support-zone width and noise thresholds; source leaves most discretionary. Any engineered threshold must be marked implementation hypothesis, not Grimes rule.
- Cost/fill model must reflect spread, adverse selection, missed limit winners, gap-through stop, latency and same-bar ordering. Close signal normally executes afterward; cannot assume known closing signal filled at earlier favorable price.
- Distinguish pattern eligibility, trigger, invalidation, stop, partials and cumulative campaign budget. Source gives no complete position-size formula or validated per-market risk percentage here.
- Validate isolated feature incrementally versus baseline, walk-forward/out-of-sample across regimes with costs, multiple-testing control and uncertainty. Book charts and historical descriptive channel table supply no BTC/India expectancy, no robust profit claim and no calibrated probability.
