## Appendix B, PDF 426–441 / print 409–424

PDF 426–427: investigate indicators with controlled synthetic inputs before interpreting market lines. A rolling SMA sees a finite window: an isolated outlier affects it on arrival and again on departure. EMA has exponentially fading memory and responds more strongly to current innovations. Initial-condition conventions matter.

PDF 428–432 (B.1–B.7): distinguish startup/transition behavior from steady trend and stabilization; SMA becomes exactly equal to a newly flat price after its window fills, EMA approaches asymptotically. A staircase price naturally converges toward its moving average during pauses; this geometry is not evidence that the average causes support. Isolated shocks, session gaps and earnings-like jumps distort derivatives of SMAs. Linear and log chart slopes have different meanings: constant percentage growth curves on a linear axis and becomes straight on a log axis.

PDF 433–435 (B.8–B.11): reproducible modified MACD is fast_t = SMA3(close)_t − SMA10(close)_t; signal_t = SMA16(fast)_t; zero reference; no histogram. This is not the ordinary EMA MACD, even with 3/10/16 inputs. A zero crossing is exactly equality of those two SMAs, not independently discovered confirmation. Signal is a lagged smoothing of fast. Standard histogram is fast minus signal; the source's phrase about subtracting lines is not consistently directional, so explicitly document the chosen sign.

Implementation convention (reviewer's explicit operational addition): use completed, ordered, contiguous bars; integer windows count observations, not clock minutes; trailing arithmetic SMA with no centered window; retain NaNs until a complete window exists. Fast first available at observation 10 and signal at observation 25. No filling missing sessions with fabricated prices. EMA comparison should state alpha and seed; commonly alpha=2/(n+1), with seed and warmup stated. These conventions are not supplied in full by Appendix B and must not be implied to be a tested author implementation.

PDF 435–438 (B.12–B.14): line curvature and hooks partly arise from rolling-window mechanics. Fast MACD can decline while price is still rising or has become flat. A one-bar spike produces several later inflections; prioritize post-shock price behavior rather than reacting to each artificial hook. PDF 439–441 (B.15–B.16): divergence can arise from smaller bar-to-bar slope OR a shorter trend leg at the same slope. It is not proof of reversal or independent order-flow evidence. Synthetic diagrams establish construction behavior, not profitability.

### Independent mathematical checks and corrections

1. PDF 428's suggestion that same-length EMA necessarily sits closer than SMA in a stable arithmetic trend is not true for conventional alpha=2/(n+1). Both asymptotic ramp lags equal (n−1)/2. Checked n=20, 2,000 unit-ramp observations: SMA lag=9.5 and EMA lag=9.5. EMA transient response still differs. Avoid copying the universal proximity claim.
2. PDF 431's wording that SMA hides cycles whose periods are whole-number multiples of its length is reversed/overbroad. For a nonzero-frequency sinusoid, an N-observation SMA nulls frequencies 2*pi*k/N (nonzero modulo 2*pi), corresponding to periods N/k, not general periods k*N. Numerical 20-SMA peaks: period10 <1.6e−14; period20 <1.2e−14; period40 about0.63531. Do not claim all high frequencies are eliminated; it is a smoothing filter with a specific frequency response.
3. PDF 435 describes the MACD fast line as measuring the second derivative. More precisely the *level* of SMA3−SMA10 is a smoothed first-difference/momentum measure; its *change* responds to changes in slope. For price=a+b*t in steady state, fast =3.5*b, not zero; unit ramp computed 3.5. The distinction prevents incorrectly equating a positive MACD level with accelerating price. PDF436's steady-ramp plateau supports this correction.
4. Correct TR for implementation is max(H−L,abs(H−previousClose),abs(L−previousClose)). Glossary PDF444 shorthand 'range plus any overnight gap' and PDF458's upward-gap example should not be generalized into adding every open gap to range. TR uses outside-range previous-close distance and also works for downward gaps.
5. PDF445 CLT explanation is imprecise: a histogram of individual fair-die outcomes does not become Gaussian with more rolls; suitably standardized sums/means approach Gaussian under conditions. Do not propagate simplistic normal-return claims.
6. PDF449 defines regression independent variables in causal language; regression association alone does not establish causation. PDF445 separately warns how hard causal identification is in markets. Prefer observed pressure/conditional relationship language over claims about hidden actor intent.
