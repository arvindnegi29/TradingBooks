# John J. Murphy, *The Visual Investor* — complete book review

## Source and reading scope

John J. Murphy, *The Visual Investor: How to Spot Market Trends*, second edition, Wiley, 2009. The reviewed source is the separately uploaded PDF “The Visual Investor - How to Spot Market Trends 2nd edition 2009.pdf” (337 physical PDF pages; 84,180 extracted words; 336 nonblank pages). The complete extracted text was reviewed in order, including all twelve chapters, conclusion, three appendices and index (physical PDF pp. 1–337). Selected chart pages were visually inspected at full-page rendering; this was not an exhaustive image-by-image audit. The examples and scan references are historical and primarily U.S.-equity oriented, with sample data ending in 2008. The book is not in the 145-PDF repository snapshot and must be counted as a separate uploaded PDF.

The contents page is physical PDF pp. 8–14. In the text of Chapter 2, a support is a prior reaction low/trough and a resistance is a prior peak; a close through a meaningful level is treated as a break, and a later successful retest can provide evidence that the broken level changed role. Those are chart observations, not assurances about the next move. Murphy says level age and repeated tests can add significance, but does not provide a calibrated scoring formula.

The source uses multiple timeframes: monthly/weekly charts for the larger trend, then shorter charts for timing. It describes uptrends through rising peaks and troughs and downtrends through falling peaks and troughs. It recommends trendlines through pivots and discusses the third touch as additional validation; channels use a parallel boundary when price action supports one. Arithmetic and logarithmic scales can make long-horizon slopes look different; use log for proportional change over long ranges and state the scale. Retracements between disclosed swing anchors include one-third, 38%, 50%, 62% and two-thirds. The book treats these as common reference fractions, not guaranteed turning points.

Chapter 3 describes pattern geometry and conventional boundary confirmation. Double/triple tops and bottoms use the intervening reaction point as a neckline/break level; head-and-shoulders formations use a neckline; triangles have converging boundaries. Pattern-height projections are approximate objectives, not guaranteed minimum returns. Its discussion of pattern-recognition software itself recognizes that visual interpretation involves subjectivity. The plugin should distinguish a developing candidate, confirmed break, adverse break, failure/bust, and an executable entry; mark anchors and knowable-at times so future pivots are not silently used early.

Volume and OBV can provide context if the provider’s volume is fit for the instrument/session. OBV is cumulative volume assigned the direction of the close-to-close move; it is not aggressor-side order flow and does not identify institutions. Divergence or heavy volume is a question to investigate, not proof of intent.

## Indicator and scan interpretation

Moving averages lag and can describe trend direction or serve as a dynamic reference. Crossovers, support/resistance touches and moving-average envelopes need a regime context. The book’s 50/200-day and 10/40-week examples are historical stock-market conventions, not defaults for BTC or Indian derivatives.

The oscillator chapters emphasize that overbought/oversold readings can persist in strong trends, while oscillators are more useful in trading ranges. The examples use RSI zones around 70/30 and midpoint 50, stochastic zones around 80/20, and longer-timeframe readings as a filter for shorter-timeframe signals. MACD uses a 12/26 EMA difference and a 9-period signal in its standard examples; its histogram describes changes in momentum and may move before a line/signal crossover. ADX readings around 20 and 40 are presented as heuristics for weak/strong trend context. Do not present any of these settings or levels as universal or validated; state actual parameters and calculate them from verified bars. Divergence is not a standalone reversal trigger.

The relative-strength ratio chapter compares one asset or group with another: a rising A/B ratio means A is outperforming B over that interval, even if A itself is falling. Check absolute direction separately. Its staged scan idea is reusable: rank a relevant universe, shortlist candidates, then inspect each actual price chart and liquidity. U.S. advance-decline lines and breadth indicators apply to broad equity universes; they are not available for an individual BTC contract and cannot be inferred from open interest or a handful of instruments.

Appendix B treats candlestick bodies and shadows as a way to display OHLC information, and discusses pattern significance in the prior trend, at chart support/resistance, and with volume context. A candle label alone is not a chart thesis. Appendix C explains the basic point-and-figure double-top breakout and double-bottom breakdown and describes stronger multi-column breaks. Murphy recommends confirming P&F patterns against bar/candlestick charts. Box size affects what the P&F display reveals, so record the chosen box/reversal settings. Do not claim that a more complex signal is more profitable absent independent testing.

## Cautions and transfer limits

The book’s fund, breadth, sector-rotation, dollar/commodity and bond/stock examples are largely drawn from U.S. data through 2008. They are historical illustrations; relationships can change and must be recalculated from current, relevant data before use on BTC or Indian markets. Appendix A’s web services and scan interfaces are dated. Its selected 2008 scans are neither live market scans nor independent evidence of expected performance.

Murphy describes Bollinger bands at two rolling standard deviations. Do not convert that into a guaranteed 95% containment statement: such a probability requires distributional assumptions that market prices do not reliably satisfy. Do not carry the book’s rhetoric that charts are always right as a claim that chart prices directly reveal fundamentals. Keep observed price behavior separate from economic interpretation. Pattern projections and named indicator thresholds do not establish an edge; only separately specified, causal and instrument-specific tests could assess that.

For live plugin use, source concepts may guide what to inspect and how to label it. Fresh Delta/Upstox evidence supplies current prices, bars, volume, options quotes and liquidity; this book supplies none of those. The book adds no automatic pattern detector, trading signal, order-flow capability, risk budget, profitability evidence or permission to execute.
