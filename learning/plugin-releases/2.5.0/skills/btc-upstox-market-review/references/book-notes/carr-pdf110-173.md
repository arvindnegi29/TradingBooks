## Coverage and original paraphrase

- PDF110–111: Part Two divider and intentional blank. PDF172–173: Part Three divider and intentional blank.
- Chapter 5, printed93–102 / PDF112–121: trend trading is a family of chart entry/exit systems, generally joining an established move rather than predicting a major top/bottom. Main discussion spans 3–30 trading days, later swing definition 5–30 days; systems may be applied intraday. Style depends on available time, temperament, skill and objectives. Source contrasts investing, position, overnight, day and scalp approaches. Preserve separation between system and holding-period style. Its annual-return figures and ease-of-income claims are author assertions, not validated targets. It does acknowledge cycles, lean periods and reserving funds. Source's suggestion that trend traders need not worry about adverse overnight gaps is not a valid operational guarantee.
- Chapter 6, printed103–111 / PDF122–130: construct a manageable candidate universe, then monitor it. Historical US-stock default is price $10–100, average daily shares >500,000, beta >2.0; sort descending beta and choose 50. Price/beta may be adapted to conditions/account needs; author explicitly refuses lowering average-share-volume floor. Use average volume rather than an isolated volume spike. Monitor daily (intraday for day trading), remove persistently erratic/choppy/gappy charts, replace from original screen, and rescreen monthly. Remove acquisition/extreme adverse-news stocks; maintain separate daily setup lists. Smoothness and gap-risk screening are qualitative. This is an old US-equity workflow, not a ready BTC/India universe rule. PDF125 mentions CCI on two-month thumbnail charts; visual PDF126–127 explicitly shows CCI(20), daily candlesticks, volume bars, and two MAs (caption identifies 20/50). No CCI formula or execution trigger is provided here.
- Chapter 7, printed113–131 / PDF132–150: instrument-specific chart remains primary; broad market is a context/efficiency aid, not a proof of any particular trade. Source uses historical SPY and QQQQ proxies, optional capitalization/sector ETFs. Do not carry obsolete tickers or historical product claims forward as current facts. Five contextual regimes below.

| Regime | Source characterization | Candidate family |
|---|---|---|
| Strong bullish | 20 MA above 50; both rising; gap large/widening; pullbacks hold 20 or zone before 50 | Long consolidation breakout; reject bearish divergence |
| Weak bullish | 20 mostly above 50; 50 rising, 20 fluctuating; gap variable; pullbacks test 50, possibly briefly beyond | Strong stock pulling to support, oversold confirmation, completed reversal candle before considering entry |
| Strong bearish | 20 below 50; both falling; gap large/widening; rallies stall at 20 or zone before 50 | Short consolidation breakdown; reject bullish divergence; abrupt squeeze reversal risk |
| Weak bearish | 20 mostly below 50; 50 falling, 20 fluctuating; gap variable; rallies test 50, possibly briefly beyond | Downtrending stock rallying to resistance, overbought confirmation, completed reversal candle before considering entry |
| Range | 50 mostly flat; 20 alternates across it; gap variable; price crosses both freely | Locate range edges, inner trendlines and indicator extremes/divergence; require intrarange trendline break for reversal |

## Chapter 8: test, transitions, causal limitations

Printed133–151 / PDF152–170 repeats five-regime rules and supplies 20 charts plus subjective author answers. All 20 charts visually inspected (PDF154–164), in addition to all text. Author explicitly calls readings subjective (PDF164/p145). Historical answer key: 1 strong bull; 2 strong bull then ambiguous transition (strong bull/strong bear or wide range, more time needed); 3 range; 4 strong bull; 5 range; 6 strong bull; 7 range; 8 range; 9 range → strong bear → range → weak bull → strong bull; 10 range; 11 strong bear; 12 weak bull → range; 13 range → strong bear; 14 weak bull → strong bull; 15 weak bull; 16 strong bear → strong bull; 17 strong bull → range; 18 strong bull → weak bull; 19 weak bear; 20 range → weak bull. Key PDF165–169 / p146–150.

Causal confirmation examples: Chart6 only confirms after pullback touches 20 and subsequently pushes off (PDF166); Chart9 sequences a relief-rally rejection at 20, declining50, later crossing50, successful support retest50, then hold20 and renewed advance (PDF166–167). Chart12 candidate bearish transition is rejected after subsequent easy recross50 (PDF167). Chart18 becomes weak after successful support at50 and subsequent rally (PDF169). These are retrospective demonstrations: a program cannot label earlier bars using a future rally. Record candidate/provisional state, confirmation bar/time and invalidation; make completed-candle observations only, with future bars withheld. A cross or touch alone does not reproduce author's reading. Chart2 explicitly needs more time. Source does not supply real-time state-machine mechanics, minimal confirmations or entry timestamp.

Tight ranges: PDF168/p149 warns that a range too narrow for trailing stops to progress is poor swing-trading terrain; seek individual charts with meaningful moves. Needs a cost/ATR/stop-based quantitative feasibility rule before implementation. Retrospective classification accuracy is not P&L validation. PDF164 claim that being right more often than wrong suffices is incomplete: expectancy also requires payoff sizes, costs, sizing and loss tails.

## Safe BTC/India transfer and missing implementation

Transfer the qualitative hierarchy: identify instrument/timeframe, establish context, choose compatible setup family, observe completed reversal/continuation confirmation, allow uncertain/transitional state and invalidation. Market context should not override missing instrument setup.

BTC24/7 daily boundaries and Indian exchange sessions need explicit candle/timezone definitions. Use appropriate fresh venue/instrument data and suitable broad/sector benchmarks for Indian equities; do not assume US proxies explain BTC or Indian indices. BTC liquidity should use executable depth/spread/notional and contract units, not500,000 US shares. India filters need appropriate rupee prices, turnover, lot size and instrument eligibility. Keep derivative contract liquidity separate from underlying trend context; futures funding/basis, options expiry/Greeks/spreads are absent here. Shortability, event gaps and trading calendars require separate handling. No threshold transfer has been empirically validated by this section.

Before any automated use specify MA type/input/warmup, slopes and tolerances, ATR-normalized separation, causal swing detection, confirmation timestamps, breach tolerance, mixed benchmark policy, range-width feasibility, actual entry/stop/exit and costs. These would be new implementation choices, not Carr's fully specified recipe. Backtest sequentially with no lookahead and realistic costs, then out-of-sample validation. These chapters provide context and universe management, not a complete trade system, execution plan, options strategy or demonstrated BTC/India edge.
