## Chapter2: chart reading (PDF58–89)

PDF60–70 printed41–51: baseline indicators are SMA20/50/200; MACD12/26/9 with histogram; stochastic5/3 (also listed5/3/3); OBV; RSI5; CCI20. Rendered Figure2.3 confirms chart label Slow STO %K(5) %D(3), not an unambiguous implementation specification for all smoothing conventions. The same period counts are proposed across weekly/daily/intraday bars (PDF71 printed52), but the examples are daily. These settings are source hypotheses, not validated BTC/India defaults.

SMA slope is used to characterize direction and relative strength and as a rough support/resistance reference (PDF60–62). MACD divergence and relative momentum are emphasized; zero-crossings are explicitly described as lagging and unreliable entries (PDF62–64). Stochastic oversold below20 followed by K crossing above D, and overbought above80 followed by K crossing below D, are described (PDF64–65); the text acknowledges overbought sells are less reliable in uptrends. OBV adds volume on up closes and subtracts on down closes; look for confirmation or divergence (PDF65–67). RSI5 uses70/30; it must be combined with price factors, and divergence is preferred over isolated threshold readings (PDF67–69). CCI20 uses extreme +200/−200 readings as possible cycle clues, not a demonstrated probability estimate (PDF69–70). Rendered PDF69 resolves extracted control-character corruption to +200/−200.

PDF71–75 printed52–56: indicators should be read together with price triggers. Persistent overbought/oversold readings can become more extreme, so threshold state alone is not entry permission. Examples juxtapose MA ordering, OBV trendline changes and indicator/price divergences. Retrospective divergence annotations are illustrations, not tested forecasting evidence; exact pivot definition, timing and confirmation lag are unspecified.

PDF76–83 printed57–64: trendlines are rough subjective visual tools. Uptrend defined through rising lows; downtrend through declining highs. Flat opposing boundaries create range; converging boundaries create triangle. Initial description allows two points, but explicit operative rule requires a THIRD touch before a trading decision (PDF81 printed62). Stops described just beyond established opposite containment line (PDF79 printed60). Closing/body prices preferred to a few wick outliers; congestion edges proposed as compromise (PDF81–82). Minimize competing lines; redraw only with awareness of subjectivity, rather than fitting desired outcome. No numeric break buffer, volatility adjustment or objective touch tolerance supplied.

PDF84–89 printed65–70: inspect watchlists each day after market context; sector sublists aid workflow; if broad direction unclear, scan both bullish/bearish possibilities. Recognize preference and ownership biases. The instruction to ignore all news/always trust chart is author advocacy and should not suppress event, corporate-action, operational or liquidity risk checks in a modern review.

## Source errors and evidentiary limits

Figure2.1 prose calls the chart SPY but actual caption/chart shows Dow Jones $INDU (PDF61). BEAS illustration text says early-November exit but rendered chart is dated5-Oct2006 and ends in October (PDF80); do not reproduce November chronology as verified. It is also a sloped descending containment channel despite text discussing a simple $2 range. The quoted +40% is a hindsight hypothetical without fills/costs/drawdown, not evidence of performance.

Indicator descriptions are conceptual, not implementation formulas: RSI paragraph describes a relative-strength ratio without supplying the final bounded RSI transform; CCI's 'mean standard deviation' wording is not a reliable precise formula; MACD moving-average type is not specified by prose. Do not translate these descriptions into code verbatim. Multiple oscillators are correlated transformations of the same prices, so agreement does not establish independent evidence. OBV accumulation/distribution is a heuristic; each executed trade has both buyer and seller. Price/volume correlations and selected stories do not prove why a subsequent move occurred.

## Chapter3: trader's mind (PDF90–100; blank101)

PDF90–95 printed71–76: author stresses discipline, responsibility, probabilistic expectations, risk awareness and willingness to be wrong. Practical takeaway is reliable execution of prewritten risk/exit rules and separating process adherence from one trade's outcome. Emotional awareness is preferred over pretending to be emotionless. Claims that psychology is the determining or primary cause of trading success/failure are unsupported generalizations here. Douglas/Tharp references are recommendations and summaries, not independent evidence that any system works. Six system variables listed: reliability, profit/loss ratio, transaction costs, trade frequency, initial account size and position sizing (PDF95 printed76).

PDF96–100 printed77–81: author proposes adapting mindfulness/values commitments to trading. Nonclinical workflow: notice urge/thought; record it as an experience rather than a fact; return to explicit plan; avoid impulse entries, averaging losses and premature winner exits. Source's ACT illness/healing claims and sweeping therapeutic statements are historical claims, not diagnostic criteria or current medical guidance. Do not call an anxious or overtrading user ill, prescribe therapy, or treat confidence as evidence of system profitability. In particular, the author's self-affirmation that his system is profitable is not a substitute for validation.

## Chapter4: ten habits (PDF102–109)

1. PDF103 printed84: minimum THREE articulable technical reasons before entry. Treat as structured rationale, not three independent votes or an automatic edge. Conflicts mean unresolved setup.
2. PDF103–104 printed84–85: losses kept bounded using exits planned before entry. Source permits mental stops under continuous observation; this is execution-dependent and does not guarantee fills.
3. PDF104 printed85: end-of-day high-watermark adjustment. New favorable closing extreme since entry moves trailing stop in favorable direction. If target-based exit used and price moves adversely to a fresh closing extreme, target moves nearer adverse price (long target lowered, short target raised). This does not license widening risk stop. Exact adjustment amount is not supplied. Source also suggests removing stops overnight and splitting targets1 cent apart; do not transplant these historical US-equity tactics, especially to24/7 BTC or exchange-limited Indian products. Removing a stop increases unprotected exposure and is not necessary for source-faithful analysis.
4. PDF104–105 printed85–86: keep all-in transaction friction low. Historical maximum$10/trade or$0.01/share benchmarks are dated, product-specific examples; modern evaluation includes spread, slippage, fees, taxes, funding and execution.
5. PDF105 printed86: author's final-two-hours US-equity entry preference and2:30pm EST institutional story are untested venue/session observations, not universal timing rules. No India/BTC clock conversion.
6. PDF105–106 printed86–87: classify weak/strong upward/downward trend or range. Weak trend -> continuation setups; strong trend -> breakouts; range -> reversals. Age of state matters; mature condition suggests shorter holding expectations. S&P50050-day SMA slope is an illustrative classifier, not quantitative weak/strong cutoff.
7. PDF106–107 printed87–88: journal date/time entry/exit, symbol, prices, size, P&L/running P&L, reasons; optionally stops/targets/sizing. Monthly setup-level review checks loss concentration, setup differences and stop fit. Avoid overfitting small samples.
8. PDF107–108 printed88–89: no adding merely to lower average cost after failure. Narrow exception is planned entry-day staged positioning on slight dip while thesis intact, far from stop, showing recovery; not unlimited averaging or increased risk after invalidation.
9. PDF108 printed89: limit trade count or reduce size/stop after predefined activity/profit threshold; resist loss recovery escalation. No numeric universal limit supplied. The author's OCD characterization of overtrading is colloquial and must not be reused as diagnosis.
10. PDF108–109 printed89–90:10% charity is personal religious/ethical advocacy, not causal evidence of profit multiplication or a required risk rule.
